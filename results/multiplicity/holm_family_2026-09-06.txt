==============================================================================
MULTIPLICITY: can the confirmatory family survive Holm-Bonferroni?
==============================================================================

  exact two-sided signed-rank floor at n=8
    enumerated over 2^8 sign flips : 0.0078125
    scipy, all-same-sign vector          : 0.0078125
    2/2^8                              : 0.0078125

  Holm's threshold for the SMALLEST p in a family of m:
    alpha/m = 0.05/18 = 0.0027778
    floor   = 0.0078125
    BELOW the floor -> nothing can be rejected

  Family as the draft describes it: 18 tests, each at the floor
    Holm-adjusted p (all tied)  : 0.1406
    rejected at alpha=0.05       : 0 of 18
    Bonferroni 18 x 0.007812   : 0.1406

  Largest family in which an n=8 exact test can survive:
    m <= alpha/floor = 0.05/0.0078125 = 6.40  ->  m_max = 6
    m= 6: alpha/m = 0.00833  survives
    m= 7: alpha/m = 0.00714  CANNOT survive
    m=18: alpha/m = 0.00278  CANNOT survive

==============================================================================
CONCLUSION
==============================================================================

  The draft's claim -- that Holm leaves every claim significant and that
  .140 applies "only for the single least extreme test" -- is wrong twice.

  1. With all 18 tests tied at the floor, Holm assigns EVERY one an
     adjusted p of 0.141, not just the least extreme. Ties propagate
     through the step-down; the largest multiplier attaches to the SMALLEST
     p-value, which is the opposite of what the sentence says.

  2. Holm therefore rejects 0 of 18. The family does not survive.

  This is structural, not a defect of these data: an n=8 exact test cannot
  produce a p below 0.00781, so any family larger than m=6 is
  unreachable at alpha=0.05 no matter how large the effect. Even a perfect
  8/8 separation with a huge effect size returns exactly 0.00781.

  The remedy is NOT to correct the claims into significance, and not to shrink
  the family post hoc to make it fit. It is to say plainly what the paper
  already half-says at the end of that same paragraph: at n=8 the exact test
  is resolution-limited, so the evidence is complete subject separation (8/8)
  and the effect size, reported alongside every p-value -- and a corrected
  p-value is not informative in this regime. That is honest and defensible;
  the current sentence is checkable and false.
