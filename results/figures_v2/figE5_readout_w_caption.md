# Extended Data Figure 5 — caption

**The readout weight cortex prefers is not the one behaviour prefers.**

The classifier's input is z(w) = w·y₁ + (1 − w)·y₂, where y₁ and y₂ are the 512-dimensional outputs of the magno and parvo streams (Methods 4.4). Results 2.4 sweeps w and reads the cue-conflict rate; here the same z(w), for w from 0 to 1 in steps of 0.05, is the design of a ridge encoding model of every voxel in NSD's general visual mask (same split, grouped folds and alpha grid as the banded model; Methods 4.5). Because z(w) sums the streams dimension by dimension, the ridge cannot undo the mixture: w constrains the design rather than re-parameterising it.

**(a)** Diet arm, three seeds averaged per subject: median test r² over an ROI's reliable voxels (noise ceiling > 0.1) as a function of w, normalised by its maximum over w, mean ± s.e.m. over eight subjects. V1 peaks near the balanced readout; hV4, ventral cortex (VO1/2, PHC1/2) and dorsal cortex (V3A/B, IPS0–2) peak on the parvo side. The curves are shallow: at the paper's w = 0.5 every ROI is within 3% of its best r², and the height differences are at the ridge's own reproducibility (Extended Data Table 12), so the result is an ordering of optima and never an r² difference.

**(b)** The optimum w* per subject (points) and its median (bar) for each region, both arms. Under the diet (blue) w* falls monotonically down the hierarchy, from 0.58 in V1 to 0.50, 0.40 and 0.35 in V2, V3 and hV4 and to 0.33–0.35 in every extrastriate group; the extrastriate mean is below V1 in 8/8 subjects (Δ = −0.25, exact Wilcoxon p = .0078) and the Spearman correlation between hierarchical rank and w* over V1–hV4 is ≤ −0.80 in every subject. Without the diet (vermillion) the optimum runs the other way, from 0.65 in V1 to 0.75–1.00 beyond hV4 (7/8 subjects, p = .023), as the luminance stream carries the image. The bottom row is the behaviour-optimal weight: the w that best reproduces each of ten human observers' trial-by-trial cue-conflict choices (trial fit of Fig. 4; diet final checkpoints, three seeds averaged), median 0.67, range 0.49–0.89. The extrastriate optima (0.27–0.40) and the observers' optima do not overlap (Mann–Whitney p = .0004).

**(c)** Both optima on the axis of the behavioural sweep: the diet network's cue-conflict shape bias as a function of w (mean of three seeds, final checkpoints). Tick marks are individual subjects (cortex) or observers (behaviour); circles mark the medians. Reading the network out with the weight extrastriate cortex prefers gives a rate of 0.56, barely above parity; the weight that best predicts human choices gives 0.78; the human rate of 0.96 lies beyond the sweep's reach. The two demands are met by the same frozen streams and differ only in w.
