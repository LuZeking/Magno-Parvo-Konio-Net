# Figure 6 — caption

**The readout account beyond the two-stream architecture.**

Every network here ends in logits = W·y + b for some penultimate feature vector y, and nothing is retrained. On one stratified half of the 1,200 cue-conflict images, y is reduced by PCA and a 16-class shape and a 16-class texture linear discriminant give fifteen shape-predictive and fifteen texture-predictive directions, made mutually orthogonal by symmetric (Löwdin) orthogonalisation: bases B_S and B_T, with B_R the residual (Methods 4.8). The curves are the pre-registered estimator, whose fitting and scored halves share object and texture exemplars; with exemplars held out the spans shrink to about 60% (Extended Data Tables 13 and 16).

**(a)** A reciprocal gain on a network's own shape- and texture-predictive subspaces moves its rate. logits(w) = W·(P_R y + 2w·P_S y + 2(1 − w)·P_T y) + b, the trained head untouched; w = 0.5 is the trained network (dot). Thin bars: the rate reachable as w runs from 0 to 1, scored on the other half of the images (10 random splits, both directions, mean). Thick segments: the part of the sweep within 5 points of the trained top-1 on held-out natural images (MiniEcoset validation for our networks, a stratified 10-per-class ILSVRC-2012 validation subset for the ImageNet networks). Grey brackets beneath: the same gated span under the pre-specified unit-level rule — a median split of the units by their shape-minus-texture η², the special case in which the two subspaces are disjoint sets of units (Extended Data Table 14). The numbers at right are the gated spans under the two rules (Extended Data Table 13). Our networks by the paper's primary estimator, the ImageNet networks by the Geirhos 16-class rule (Methods 4.3). In the six ImageNet networks the whole sweep lies inside the gate (top-1 changes by at most 1.5 points) and the rate moves by 0.25–0.33, monotonically (ρ ≥ 0.997; 0.14–0.20 with exemplars held out); in our three single trunks the 16-class heads lose 7–15 points of top-1 at the ends of the sweep and the gated spans are 0.14–0.22; the unit-level rule reaches 0.07–0.16. In the two-stream network the discriminant subspaces are not the streams (63–70% of the shape basis's energy lies on stream 1) and the subspace gain moves the rate by 0.32 (0.50 → 0.80; 0.25 with exemplars held out), where the streams themselves, which the unit-level rule recovers, give 0.46 (grey) and the readout weight of Fig. 4a 0.50. Grey band, the ten human observers (0.92–0.99).

**(b)** The same curves for the six ImageNet networks (top) and the change of top-1 along each sweep (bottom; grey band, the five-point budget). Dots are the trained rates; the field's ranking is not invariant to the readout. Within the budget five of the six networks can be moved past the trained rates of four of the other five (ViT-B/16, the highest, past one), and the six can be placed in the reverse of their trained order; with exemplars held out four of the six still pass at least two others' trained rates, but the reverse order is no longer reachable (Results 2.4).

**(c)** Which component carries cortex. For the single trunks the two unit halves of the pre-specified rule are the two bands of a banded ridge model of every NSD voxel (eight subjects, Methods 4.5); the index (U_S − U_T)/(U_S + U_T) is the median over reliable voxels per region, mean ± s.e.m. over subjects (seeds averaged per subject). The two-stream diet model's magno − parvo profile (Fig. 3, Extended Data Fig. 4) is drawn on the same axis. In the no-diet single trunk the texture-leaning half carries every extrastriate group relative to V1 (8/8 subjects, Δ = −0.32, exact p = .0078) with no descent within V1–hV4; the diet-trained single ResNet-34 shows the V1-relative contrast (7/8, p = .016), a descent already within V1–hV4 (7/8, p = .016) and ventral cortex as its most texture-carried region (dorsal − ventral +0.16, 8/8); in ImageNet ResNet-50, whose units are almost all texture-leaning, that half carries V1 through hV4 and ventral cortex alike (−0.32 to −0.50) while lateral and dorsal cortex are balanced, so the V1-relative contrast reverses (+0.24, p = .039). In ConvNeXt-B and ViT-B/16, fitted after the pre-registered analysis, the texture-leaning half carries every region (index −0.09 to −0.45 and −0.13 to −0.31) with no V1-relative contrast (+0.22, 2/8 subjects negative; −0.08, 6/8, p = .25). Unique variances of the two halves in the single trunks and ConvNeXt-B are 0.006–0.04 (ViT-B/16 up to 0.09), against 0.10–0.25 for the parvo band with the split; most of a single trunk's explained variance is shared between its halves (Extended Data Table 15).

**(d)** The mixture each region prefers. The lossy sum z(w) = w·y_S + (1 − w)·y_T (halves paired by rank) is the design of a ridge model at 11 values of w (0, 0.1, …, 1), following Extended Data Fig. 5; points are subjects, bars medians, symbols as in (c). In the no-diet trunk the optimum falls from 0.80 in V1 to 0.37–0.48 beyond hV4 (7/8, p = .023); in the diet ResNet-34 the optimum sits near 0.55 in every region (the curves stay within 5% of their maximum over w in all three networks, as in Extended Data Fig. 5); in ResNet-50, V1–hV4 and ventral cortex prefer the texture-leaning half (0.0–0.1) while lateral and dorsal cortex prefer the shape-leaning one (0.7–0.85); ConvNeXt-B prefers the texture-leaning half in every region (median 0.0) and ViT-B/16 nearly so (0.10–0.25). Prediction P5 therefore holds in one of the three pre-specified networks and in neither of the two added after them.
