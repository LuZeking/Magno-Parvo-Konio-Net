# Figure 5 — caption

**Three properties the field treats as travelling with shape bias do not follow from it.**

**(a)** Out-of-distribution robustness. Corruptions are applied to the image before the front end and scored in the models' own 112-class label space at each seed's best saved checkpoint; plotted is the area under the curve of accuracy relative to each seed's own clean accuracy, one marker per seed, for the diet (blue) and no-diet (vermillion) arms, with the swap and `parvo_highsf` controls as small squares. ✱ marks complete seed separation (the exact 3 v 3 Mann–Whitney floor, p = 0.100); ≈ marks the one family where the arms are equivalent to within ±0.033. The diet-trained model is less robust on four of the five corruption families it was not trained on and more robust on exactly one family, low-pass, which the diet installs. Shaded rows are the two families the diet has effectively seen; it is not more robust to contrast reduction even though its front end includes a contrast-sensitivity cut. Full degradation curves: Extended Data Fig. 1.

**(b)** Trial-level agreement with the same ten human observers (error consistency, Cohen's κ, as a percentage of the human–human ceiling of κ = 0.234 over 45 observer pairs) against cue-conflict shape-bias rate, for all 189 readouts of the w sweep (9 runs × 21 weights) and the three published CNNs (Geirhos et al. 2020). The rate spans 3×; agreement never exceeds 20.7% of the ceiling even with w chosen post hoc, per run, to maximise it. No regression line is drawn, because the x-position of every point was set by a free parameter.

**(c)** Positive control. On the same 1,200 images and the same 10 observers, with shape- and texture-category effects removed and w chosen on half the images and scored on the held-out half, the same machinery recovers 24.1% of the split-half ceiling for whether an image resolves to either cue at all against 8.7% for which cue is picked (diet arm; three seeds as dots, bar = mean). Each target is expressed against its own split-half ceiling.

**(d)** The crossed front-end 2×2 that withdraws the diet reading of (c). Difficulty correspondence for diet weights and no-diet weights evaluated with the developmental front end on and off. Both train/test-matched cells are positive (24.1%, 9.2%) and both crossed cells are not (−0.6%, −3.7%), so the diet arm's advantage in (c) is a train/test-matching pattern, not an effect of the learned weights.
