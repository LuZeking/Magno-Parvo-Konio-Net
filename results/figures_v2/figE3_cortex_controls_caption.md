# Extended Data Figure 3 — caption

**The cortical gradient against its nuisance axes: the raw statistic, eccentricity and receptive-field size.**

**(a, b)** The stream-preference profile of Fig. 3a on both statistics: (a) the normalised per-voxel index (U_m − U_p)/(U_m + U_p), median over reliable voxels per ROI, and (b) the raw per-voxel difference U_m − U_p in units of noise-ceiling-normalised r². Lines are means over 8 subjects, shading ± SEM; the trained arms average three seeds per subject, the untrained network on the diet front end is a single model. The diet arm's normalised index approaches −0.84 at hV4, near the index's bound of −1; the raw statistic carries the same ordering (V1 near zero, hV4 at −0.16) without a bound, and the two-statistics rule of Methods 4.7 requires every claim to hold on both.

**(c)** The gradient hV4 − mean(V1v, V1d) computed within each of NSD's five eccentricity bands (`prf-eccrois`), so that V1 and hV4 voxels are compared only with voxels of matched eccentricity. One point per subject per band (seed-mean for the trained arms), bars = mean; a cell needs at least 20 voxels per ROI or the subject is dropped from that band. Under the diet the gradient is negative in every band (6/7 subjects in the foveal band, 8/8 in the other four) and grows with eccentricity, from −0.26 in the foveal band to −0.93 beyond 4°; without the diet it stays between −0.10 and +0.07 in every band. Labels give the number of subjects in which the diet arm is more negative than the no-diet arm and the exact Wilcoxon p for that band. Eccentricity therefore modulates the diet arm's gradient but does not produce it.

**(d)** The same gradient within pRF-size tertiles, boundaries set per subject on the pooled V1 + hV4 voxels using NSD's pRF size estimates (voxels with pRF R² > 5%). Under the diet the gradient is negative in every tertile (−0.71, −0.77, −0.90; all subjects), without the diet it is near zero in every tertile (−0.08 each), so the hierarchical contrast is not a receptive-field-size contrast in disguise. The within-subject regressions of Extended Data Table 9 give the same picture on the continuous predictors.
