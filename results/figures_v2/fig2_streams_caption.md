# Figure 2 — caption

**The two streams end training on opposite sides of the shape–texture boundary, and they get there by different routes.**

**(a)** Linear-probe shape−texture bias of each stream's features on exemplars the probe was not trained on — shape-label accuracy minus texture-label accuracy on the Geirhos cue-conflict images, from a probe trained on three exemplars per class for both labels with one exemplar left out per fold and PCA and scaling fitted inside each fold, mean of 20 draws (Methods 4.3); zero is the sign flip — before and after training, with the diet (solid) and without it (dashed). The untrained references are single models with no training seed; trained points are the mean of three seeds with the seed range as a bar. Under the diet the magno stream is already shape-biased before any weight is updated (+0.21) and training adds +0.13; the parvo stream starts near neutral (−0.03) and acquires its texture bias over training (−0.17). Without the diet, training drives the luminance stream from +0.09 across the sign flip to −0.12: ordinary training removes the shape bias the input supplies. The parvo stream ends texture-biased whether or not the other stream is dieted (−0.12 without). Decoding new exemplars is hard — the diet's magno stream reads a new exemplar's shape at 0.41–0.42 and its texture at 0.08, against a chance level of 0.0625 — so the biases are smaller than with the paper's original stratified-fold probe, whose test images share object and texture exemplars with its training folds; that probe gives every direction drawn here (magno +0.80 untrained and +0.82 trained under the diet, parvo −0.09 and −0.52, the undieted luminance stream +0.42 and −0.23; Table 1).

**(b, c)** The carrier × transform 2×2 on the held-out probe (b) and on cue-conflict shape bias (c). Each line is one carrier (chromatic L−M or achromatic luminance); the x-axis is whether the developmental transform is applied to it. Points are the three training seeds; the thick line is their mean. Neither factor alone crosses the boundary; only the conjunction does, on both instruments (probe: chromatic carrier −0.118 without the transform and −0.002 with it, achromatic carrier −0.139 and +0.337; interaction +0.36, and +0.73 with exemplars shared; Supplementary Table S3). Cells are read out on stream 2 in three cases and on the diet's magno stream in the fourth (Methods).

**(d)** How each readout decides the 1,200 cue-conflict images at the best saved checkpoint (diet) and the final checkpoint (no diet): images decided as the shape category, as the texture category, or as neither of the two. Shape bias is shape/(shape + texture); the number of decided images varies about four-fold across readouts, so rates near parity are read as “no strong preference”. A forced two-way estimator that keeps every image (shape logit against texture logit) compresses all rates toward 0.5 but preserves every ordering shown here (Supplementary Table S9).

**(e, f)** Accuracy-matched control. The diet-trained models are less accurate, so the conjunction effect could in principle be an accuracy effect. A no-diet network retrained with per-epoch checkpointing and stopped, per seed, at the epoch nearest the diet's accuracy (epochs 10, 10 and 11; 0.546–0.554 against 0.556) closes 4.3% of the magno stream's probe gap on held-out exemplars (e; +0.33 diet at its best checkpoint, −0.12 no diet, −0.10 matched; 1.5–5.9% across three seeds, 4.1–8.0% under the other held-out probe variants, 7.2% and 5.8–8.1% with exemplars shared) and 6.1% of the behavioural gap (f; 2.2–9.1%), and its magno stream stays on the texture side in every seed (−0.10 to −0.12). Three matched seeds, as for the diet and no-diet arms. The six checkpoints of this control were added to the held-out probe after the pre-registration, as a descriptive extension with no prediction (`docs/PLAN_EXEMPLAR_2026-09-21.md`, addendum of 2026-09-22; `results/figures_v2/exemplar_matched.json`).
