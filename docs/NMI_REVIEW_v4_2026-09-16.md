## Consensus Weaknesses

1. **[Abstract; Results 2.4 cortex paragraph; Fig. 6c; Limitations]** The abstract's cortical generality clause — "in our single trunks extrastriate cortex loads on the texture-leaning units" — carries none of the qualifications the Results attach to it: it holds under the unit rule only (the pre-specified subspace rule's cortical predictions failed in 4 of 5 families), in the authors' two trunks and not in ImageNet ResNet-50 (extrastriate − V1 = +0.24, p = .039), on unique variances of ≤ 0.04 with 80–96% of the explained variance shared between the halves, and the paper itself concludes that "which component cortex loads on is decided by how the representation is partitioned". Under those conditions the cortical clause is a result about the two-stream architecture and the authors' own trunks, and the abstract should say so.
2. **[§2.2, Fig. 2e–f, ED Table 10; §2.3, Fig. 3a, ED Tables 1 and 11; Supp. Note S2; Methods 4.5]** Three n = 1 cells remain and the stated reason for the two cortical ones does not survive the repository. Methods 4.5: "each subject's two-band fit was submitted at about 160 GB of memory, and that allocation was not available to us for the two controls". `slurm/encode_cc_controls.sh` (2026-09-15) documents the actual cause (himalaya's banded search forming a 21 × n_test × n_train float64 matrix), the fix (`n_alphas_batch = 7`, no change of estimate, equivalence probe at r = 1.000 against the stored diet fit), the measured footprint (26.7 GB → 34 GB request) and a 32-element array for exactly these seeds. The accuracy-matched control's seeds 1–2 already exist in the tree (`results/cue_conflict_matched/…seed1_ep010.json`, `…seed2_ep011.json`) and change the reported number (7.0% → 6.1% at n = 3). 3. **[Code availability; README "Verify the reported numbers"; `CITATION.cff`]** The command the README gives as the "fastest real check" ends `FAILURES: ['matched closes % of behavioural gap']` on the tree as supplied, against the README's promised `FAILURES: none`; the tree and the PDF are out of step. `CITATION.cff` has a placeholder author entry and no DOI; the README's stage-2 footprint ("≈ 160G per task") and its explanation of the n = 1 controls are contradicted by `encode_cc_controls.sh` in the same repository; the `requirements.txt` header names the previous title and `PAPER_DRAFT.md`.

### Summary of key messages
A two-stream network receiving the luminance and L−M channels of a cone-opponent image, with a developmental blur and contrast-sensitivity cut on the luminance channel, ends training with a shape-biased magno stream — supplied by the input and protected by the diet — and a texture-biased parvo stream that is learnt. Fitted to eight NSD subjects, the texture-biased stream's unique variance carries every extrastriate region, with a V1 → hV4 gradient that is the most extreme of 630 labellings in every seed and that training without the diet erodes. Behaviour reads out from both streams: one weight over frozen features moves the cue-conflict rate from 0.40 to 0.90, a refitted nonlinear head preserves the span, and the weight extrastriate cortex prefers (≈ 0.33) is not the weight human choices prefer (0.67). In 38 frozen ImageNet classifiers with no stream split, one gain on the network's own shape-predictive subspace — fifteen discriminant directions, head untouched — moves the rate by 0.20–0.50 within five points of top-1, the published ranking and the shape-end ranking agree only at τ = 0.47, and a probe of the representation ranks the zoo differently again (τ = 0.44); the Stylized-ImageNet ResNet-50 is the exception whose bias sits in the representation. In the authors' two single trunks, extrastriate cortex loads on the texture-leaning half of the units; this did not hold in ImageNet ResNet-50, and the two discriminant subspaces carry almost no unique cortical variance in any network. Robustness, trial-level agreement with humans and dimensionality do not follow from the rate. The authors conclude that the cue-conflict rate is a readout statistic and propose three numbers every shape-bias claim should carry.
