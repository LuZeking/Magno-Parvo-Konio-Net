using a biologically motivated two-stream network, tested against human 7T fMRI

the result that would matter most to NMI's readership — that the field's most

## Consensus Strengths

1. **The controls are run against the authors' own hypotheses and reported when they fail.** Two pre-registered predictions failed (the inverted-front-end prediction and the error-consistency prediction) and are in the main text; the diet's costs (accuracy, late-training instability, BOLD prediction, robustness) each get a panel; a positive result the authors could have claimed (the diet's difficulty correspondence, Fig. 5c) is withdrawn by their own 2×2 in the next panel.
2. **Attribution is by construction.** Bit-identical trunk initialisation and the same-image control make every stream difference attributable to input, which is what lets the carrier × transform 2×2 (Fig. 2b, c) carry causal weight.
3. **The cortical instrument answers the question the thesis's RSA could not.** Variance partitioning separates a global stream preference from regional structure, and the paper shows both: the arms sit on opposite sides of the global axis and only one has a gradient (Fig. 3a). The exact 630-labelling null (Fig. 3c) is the right test for a seven-ROI ordering.
4. **The figures lead.** Each figure carries one claim; panel letters, colours and checkpoint policy are consistent; legends state n and the replication unit.
5. **The affine identity is disclosed rather than sold.** The w sweep is stated to be exact interpolation; the refitted linear and nonlinear heads (Fig. 4b) are the right response to the obvious objection, and the accuracy-gated span is reported alongside.

## Consensus Weaknesses

1. **[Data/code availability; whole paper]** Code central to every claim is "in the accompanying repository" with no location, licence, version or DOI, and is not available to the referee. Under NMI policy this alone blocks acceptance.
2. **[§2.3, Fig. 3]** The headline cortical result is not shown to survive the two nuisance axes that co-vary with hierarchical rank: **eccentricity / pRF size** and **noise ceiling**. The text says the noise-ceiling floor was simulated and points the wrong way, but gives no numbers by ROI; it says nothing about eccentricity, although V1→hV4 is also a gradient of receptive-field size and the blurred magno stream is, by construction, a low-spatial-frequency instrument. The earlier draft reported an eccentricity-matched contrast (−0.736, 8/8) and a pRF-size stratification; this manuscript must carry them.
3. **[§2.3, Fig. 3a]** Under the diet the normalised index approaches −0.84 at hV4, close to the −1 bound. A gradient measured on a bounded ratio whose one end is near saturation needs the raw statistic shown beside it, per ROI, not only summarised in a sentence.
4. **[§2.3; Supplementary Note S1]** The gradient is the survivor of seven regional hypotheses; six failed. The paper says so, which is creditable, but the family-wise consequence is left to the reader. State the selection explicitly at the point of claim and report the gradient as a post hoc finding with the exact null as calibration, not as a confirmation.
5. **[§2.2, Fig. 2d; Methods 4.3]** 58–77% of cue-conflict images are decided as neither the shape nor the texture category, so the shape-bias rate is computed on roughly a third of the set and the models' accuracy on these stimuli is low (shape accuracy ≈ 0.26 at w = 0.5). The rate's dependence on the decided subset is drawn but not quantified; report shape bias under a forced two-way choice (shape vs texture logit only) as a second estimator, and report the models' shape and texture accuracies as the thesis did.
6. **[§2.4, Fig. 4a–b]** The generality of "the rate is a property of the readout" is demonstrated inside one architecture with two oppositely biased components. The ML reader will ask whether the same holds for a standard single-trunk CNN — for example, does a linear readout re-fitted on a frozen ResNet's penultimate features move the rate across a comparable range when the shape- and texture-predictive subspaces are re-weighted? Without that, the claim's scope is the two-stream model.
7. **[Single-seed arms throughout]** The cortical fits for `swap` and `parvo_highsf`, the accuracy-matched control, the koniocellular control and the thesis checkpoint are each one run. The text labels them n = 1, correctly, but several load-bearing sentences rest on them (the "only the diet on luminance yields two streams that both carry unique variance" statement; the schedule-vs-static equivalence).
8. **[Title]** "a texture-biased ventral map" asserts a property of human ventral cortex that the paper explicitly does not claim (Discussion: "by role, not by content"). The title should name the model's property, not cortex's.
9. **[Extended Data]** The per-seed numbers in the text have no tables in this manuscript. Seven Extended Data tables are needed: cortical gradient (seed × statistic), monotonicity and exact-null p per seed, in-silico cue conflict per seed, the 2×2 cells with seed ranges and decided-image counts, single-stream controls, OOD AUC per family and condition, and the seven-model error-consistency table.
10. **[Statements]** Author contributions, competing interests, ethics / approvals for secondary use of human data, and a reporting summary are absent.
11. **[Abstract]** 324 words against a ≈ 150–200-word norm, and it contains eleven numbers; the Nature-portfolio abstract should carry the claim and one or two anchors.

## Comments to Authors (Nature-style, no recommendation here)
==========================================

### Summary of key messages

The authors build a two-stream convolutional network whose two ResNet-18 trunks
receive, respectively, the luminance and the L−M chromatic channel of a
cone-opponent image, with a fixed "developmental visual diet" (blur plus a
contrast-sensitivity cut matched to 48 months) applied to the luminance channel.
They report that (i) the two streams end training with opposite shape–texture
biases, the magno stream's shape bias being supplied by the input and preserved
by the diet while the parvo stream's texture bias is learnt, and that the shape
bias requires the conjunction of an achromatic carrier with the transform;
(ii) fitted as voxel-wise encoding models to eight NSD subjects, the
texture-biased stream's unique variance rises from V1 to hV4 along a gradient
that is the most extreme of all 630 labellings of the seven ROIs in every seed,
and that training without the diet erodes; (iii) the integrated behavioural
readout is shape-biased, the readout weight moves the rate from 0.40 to 0.90
over frozen representations, and a refitted nonlinear head preserves the span;
(iv) robustness, agreement with human trial-level decisions and representational
dimensionality do not follow from the rate. The authors frame this as an
existence proof that shape-biased behaviour does not require shape-biased
representations along the ventral pathway, and are explicit that the mapping to
cortex is by explanatory role rather than content.

### Major comments

1. **Code availability (all results).** The manuscript states that code and
   derived tables are "in the accompanying repository" but gives no location,
   licence, version or DOI, and nothing was available to this referee. Every
   headline number depends on custom code (front end, banded ridge with variance
   partitioning, cue-conflict scoring, the exact permutation null). Please
   provide the repository to referees now, with a tagged version, a licence, an
   environment manifest and the committed result files from which the figures
   are regenerated — including the per-subject encoding outputs that Fig. 3
   reads.

2. **The cortical gradient and its nuisance axes (§2.3, Fig. 3a–c).** The
   claim is that the parvo stream's explanatory share rises toward hV4. Two
   variables rise along the same axis: receptive-field size / eccentricity
   coverage, and the drop in noise ceiling. The magno stream is a low-pass
   instrument by construction, so a stream-preference profile that follows
   receptive-field size would be expected from the front end alone. The paper
   controls the noise-ceiling floor by simulation (a sentence) but says nothing
   about eccentricity or pRF size. Please report the gradient within eccentricity
   bands and stratified by pRF size, and show the simulated floor per ROI. The
   claim in the Discussion that the gradient "is not eccentricity" needs to be a
   result, not an assertion.

3. **Saturation of the normalised index (§2.3, Fig. 3a).** Under the diet the
   index reaches −0.84 at hV4 on a scale bounded at −1. The raw statistic is
   said to agree, and the two-statistics rule is a sound practice, but the raw
   profile is not drawn. Please show it per ROI beside the normalised one so the
   reader can judge how much of the gradient's shape is bounded.

4. **Post hoc status of the gradient (§2.3; Supplementary Note S1).** The
   authors disclose that six of seven regional hypotheses failed and that the
   gradient is the survivor. That disclosure should sit at the point of claim in
   §2.3, and the exact permutation null should be presented as a calibration of
   the survivor's extremity, not as a confirmation of a prediction.

5. **The decided-image denominator (§2.2, Fig. 2d; Methods 4.3).** Between 58%
   and 77% of the 1,200 cue-conflict images are classified as neither the shape
   nor the texture category, so the reported rates are computed on roughly a
   third of the set, and the models' accuracy on these stimuli is low. Please
   add a forced two-way estimator (shape logit vs texture logit) for the 2×2
   cells, the w sweep and the human comparison, and report shape and texture
   accuracies per readout as the originating thesis did. If the interaction of
   Fig. 2b–c and the span of Fig. 4a survive the forced choice, the claims are
   considerably strengthened; if they do not, that is itself the finding.

6. **Scope of "the rate is a property of the readout" (§2.4, Fig. 4).** Inside
   a network built with two oppositely biased components, that a convex
   combination of them spans the rate is close to definitional, as the authors
   say. Its interest to the wider readership depends on whether it also holds
   for ordinary networks. Please test a frozen single-trunk network: re-weight
   shape- and texture-predictive subspaces of its penultimate features (or the
   two single-stream controls' features) and report the reachable span.

7. **Single-seed arms.** The `swap` and `parvo_highsf` cortical fits, the
   accuracy-matched control, the koniocellular control and the thesis's
   schedule-trained checkpoint are each one run. The manuscript labels them
   correctly, but the statement that "only the diet on luminance yields two
   streams that both carry substantial unique cortical variance" rests on two of
   them. Two further seeds for the two cortical controls would settle it.

8. **Title.** "a texture-biased ventral map" ascribes to human cortex a
   property the Discussion says is not claimed. A title that names the model's
   property — e.g. that the texture-biased stream carries the ventral end of the
   model–cortex mapping — would match the text.

9. **Extended Data.** The per-seed values quoted in the text have no tables in
   the manuscript. Please add tables for: the cortical gradient per seed and
   statistic; monotonicity and exact-null p per seed; in-silico cue conflict per
   seed; the 2×2 cells with seed ranges and decided counts; the single-stream
   controls; OOD AUC per family and condition; the seven-model error-consistency
   table.

10. **Required statements.** Ethics/approvals for the secondary use of the two
    human datasets, competing interests, author contributions and a reporting
    summary are missing.

### Minor comments

- m1. Abstract: 324 words with eleven numbers; cut to ≈ 200 with the claim and one or two anchors.
- m2. Fig. 1c legend and §2.1: "best saved checkpoint 0.556" and "final-epoch mean 0.445" — state once, in one place, which policy each later figure uses; the reader currently reassembles it from legends.
- m3. Fig. 2a: the untrained references are single models; say in the legend that the seed range bars belong to the trained end only (it does) and give the untrained model's identity.
- m4. Fig. 3b: the legend gives per-subject seed means (−0.79/−0.07) and per-seed values (−0.834/−0.785/−0.745); make clear these are the same quantity aggregated two ways.
- m5. Fig. 4c: the published CNNs are placed at an arbitrary x; a marginal strip would avoid the appearance of an accuracy value.
- m6. §2.4: "86% of the 0.540 affine baseline registered before the run" — the registered baseline and the measured affine span differ (0.540 vs 0.508); one sentence on why.
- m7. §2.5, robustness: the equivalence bound for phase scrambling rests on a Welch interval at 2.3 degrees of freedom; say so in the main text.
- m8. Methods 4.5: give the number of voxels per ROI after the noise-ceiling threshold, per subject, in a table.
- m9. Methods 4.2: hardware, GPU-hours and an energy estimate for the 21 training runs.
- m10. References: Geirhos et al. (2019) is cited for the shape-bias/robustness coupling in the Discussion's source; the Results cite 2018a and 2021 — align.
- m11. "Developmental visual diet" is a fixed 48-month transform here; consider "developmental-diet snapshot" or state the fixity in the abstract.

### Notes by criterion

- **Technical soundness.** Methods are appropriate and the two-statistics rule, exact tests and pre-registration are exemplary practice; the missing eccentricity/pRF and saturation analyses (major 2–3) are the gap between good and excellent.
- **Support for conclusions.** Strong for the stream-divergence and readout claims; adequate for the cortical claim pending major 2–4; single-seed arms limit the control statements.
- **Significance and originality.** The conceptual move — behaviour as a readout over components, demonstrated with cortical grounding — is genuine, and the honest accounting of what the diet costs is rare. The measurement consequence for the field's alignment metric is the most broadly relevant result and is under-placed.
- **Methodology and statistics.** n = 8 exact tests at their floor; equivalence bounds pre-fixed; no correction claimed and the reason given. Acceptable and clearly stated.
- **Reproducibility.** Not assessable from the manuscript; see major 1.
- **Clarity and presentation.** Figures are excellent; the text is dense with numbers where tables would serve; abstract too long.
- **Responsible AI.** Human data are public and de-identified but the manuscript does not say so; no dual-use concern; energy cost unreported.
