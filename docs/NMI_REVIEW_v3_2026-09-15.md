## Comments to Authors (Nature-style, no recommendation here)
==========================================

### Summary of key messages
A two-stream network receiving luminance and L−M channels of a cone-opponent image, with a developmental blur and contrast-sensitivity cut on the luminance channel, ends training with a shape-biased magno stream (supplied by the input, preserved by the diet) and a texture-biased parvo stream (learnt). Fitted to eight NSD subjects, the texture-biased stream's unique variance carries every extrastriate region, with a V1 → hV4 gradient that is the most extreme of all 630 labellings in every seed and that training without the diet erodes. Behaviour reads out from both streams; one weight over frozen features moves the cue-conflict rate from 0.40 to 0.90, a refitted nonlinear head preserves the span, and the weight cortex prefers (≈ 0.33) is not the weight human choices prefer (0.67). In nine frozen single-trunk networks, including six ImageNet models, a gain on the network's own shape-leaning units moves the rate monotonically by 0.09–0.21 — enough to reorder the field's ranking — and in the authors' two single trunks extrastriate cortex again loads on the texture-leaning units relative to V1, though not in ImageNet ResNet-50. Robustness, trial-level agreement with humans and dimensionality do not follow from the rate. The authors conclude that the cue-conflict rate is a readout statistic.

### Major comments
1. **Magnitude of the generality claim (Abstract; Title; Discussion).** The abstract says "ten frozen networks without a stream split" where Results 2.4 and the Fig. 6 caption say nine, and "reorders the field's ranking at no accuracy cost" where Results says "almost" and the re-orderings in the Fig. 6 caption are read at w = 1, outside the 0.15–0.90 window within which ED Table 13 keeps the ResNets' top-1 within 5 points. Please state each re-ordering at the gated w and carry the qualifier into the abstract. More broadly, the single-trunk spans (0.09–0.21 fixed-head; 0.05–0.10 refit-robust) missed the pre-stated thresholds (0.20; 0.15) in every network, and the Discussion says so; the abstract and title do not. The reader of the abstract should learn that the readout account holds in kind in single trunks and at a third to a half of the two-stream magnitude, with the refit-robust part smaller still.
2. **Single-seed cortical controls (§2.3; Fig. 3a; ED Tables 1 and 11).** The statement that only the diet on luminance yields two streams that both carry unique cortical variance rests on the swap and `parvo_highsf` fits at seed 0. Please add seeds 1–2, or state why not; in either case mark n = 1 in Results 2.3 and in the column headers of ED Table 11, whose caption presently says trained arms are seed means.
3. **Checkpoint policy (Table 1; §2.4; Methods 4.7).** Table 1 quotes final-checkpoint behaviour (0.878 / 0.418 / 0.675) without saying so; Fig. 4c and ED Table 5 quote best saved (0.903 / 0.400 / 0.655); §2.4 gives integration costs of 0.203 and 0.248 three paragraphs apart. Please choose one policy per section and state it at the point of use.
4. **Arithmetic in §2.4.** 0.466 / 0.508 = 0.92, not 86%; 0.760 − 0.421 = 0.339, not 0.283. Please state which baseline the 86% refers to and which head the endpoints belong to.
5. **Split rule (Methods 4.8).** With 0–2% of units shape-leaning in absolute terms in the ImageNet networks, the "shape-leaning half" is a relative label. Please report the fixed-head sweep with S = {d_j > 0} and with a top-decile split, from the stored features, so the reader can see whether the span is a property of the median rule.
6. **Reproducibility of the new analyses (ED Tables 13–14; Code availability).** The two new tables cite cluster paths; the verification script named in Code availability predates the analyses and checks none of their numbers; the trained checkpoints that Figs. 4 and 6 read are not named in any availability statement. Please source the tables to repository files, extend the verification script, and deposit the checkpoints with the DOI release.
