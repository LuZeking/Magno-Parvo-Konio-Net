## Abstract

Humans name objects by shape when shape and texture conflict, yet the ventral stream is well described by texture statistics. We model the subcortical split as two ResNet-18 trunks fed luminance and L−M under one objective. When the luminance input matures from infant to adult during training, its stream learns a shape bias; with adult input throughout, or the ramp on L−M, both lean to texture. Only this conjunction divides the cortical hierarchy of eight Natural Scenes Dataset subjects: the shape-biased stream carries V1, the texture-leaning stream extrastriate cortex. Only it tracks which images humans resolve (22% of the ceiling, against 8–9% for both controls on identical input). One readout weight moves its shape-bias rate from 0.48 to 0.85. Predicted cortex, however, is not texture-biased on the cue-conflict images themselves (shape-bias rates 0.47–0.53); in the model the bias is set by the readout over both components.

## 1. Introduction

Humans presented with an image whose shape and texture point to different categories name the shape: on the cue-conflict images of Geirhos et al. (2018a), ten observers did so on 96% of decided trials, while ImageNet-trained convolutional networks named the texture. Closing that gap is a standard target for biologically motivated models (Hermann et al., 2020; Jang & Tong, 2021); training on degraded early input, as infancy supplies it (Vogelsang et al., 2018, 2024), has been studied as a route to more human-like recognition (Avberšek et al., 2021; Jinsi et al., 2023; Yoshihara et al., 2023; Jang & Tong, 2024), and a developmental visual diet — input whose acuity, contrast and chromatic sensitivity follow the human developmental trajectory — is among the most effective routes to it (Lu et al., 2026).

A second body of evidence sits uneasily beside the first: ventral-stream representations are well described by texture statistics — category-selective human cortex fails to discriminate natural images from texture-matched scrambles yet still discriminates categories (Jagadeesh & Gardner, 2022), macaque V4 responses to natural textures are predicted by their image statistics (Okazawa et al., 2015), and in inferotemporal cortex texture is the more decodable cue (Jagadeesh & Livingstone, 2024; Komatsu & Goda, 2018). The argument needs only that the ventral description is substantially texture-like, not that shape is absent (the evidence is one human study and several macaque ones).

If behaviour is shape-biased and the ventral stream texture-biased, either the two measures disagree about what shape bias is, or behaviour is not a readout of one representation. The second horn is a hypothesis about architecture that single-trunk models cannot express, and two facts make it concrete: the visual system receives a luminance-dominated, coarse magnocellular signal and a chromatically opponent, finer parvocellular one, separated before cortex and later recombined (Livingstone & Hubel, 1987; Kaplan & Shapley, 1982; Derrington et al., 1984; Nassi & Callaway, 2009; Ferrera et al., 1994), and that input is not stationary over development. The two-stream hypothesis (Mishkin et al., 1983; Goodale & Milner, 1992) and the emergence of ventral- and dorsal-like streams in a two-pathway network trained on one objective (Bakhtiari et al., 2021) motivate an architecture whose streams are defined by their subcortical-like inputs.

Two-stream networks reproduce the dorsal–ventral division (Choi et al., 2023; Finzi et al., 2026; see also Dobs et al., 2022). Closest to our question, blurry, achromatic training before sharp, colour training made one network's classification more shape-biased, attributed to magnocellular-like units (Vogelsang et al., 2025); here the split is set by two streams' inputs, the input matures continuously to adulthood, and a control on identical test input, a channel swap and cortical encoding test the account.

This paper asks two questions, in the order the originating study posed them (Manocchio, 2026): can two streams fed magnocellular- and parvocellular-like inputs diverge while optimising a single objective and producing shape-biased behaviour, and does such a system explain how the ventral stream can be texture-like while behaviour is shape-biased? We build it — two ResNet-18 trunks fed the luminance and L−M channels of a cone-opponent image, the luminance channel under a developmental diet that matures from birth to adulthood over training (Fig. 1) — and examine its streams with probes and cue-conflict behaviour (Fig. 2), encoding models of eight subjects' 7T fMRI responses (Fig. 3) and the readout that combines them (Fig. 4). The streams diverge by different routes, and the shape-biased one only when its input is achromatic and matured during training: the same network shown the adult input from the first step, or given the ramp on its L−M channel instead, learns two texture-leaning streams. Only that conjunction divides the cortical hierarchy — the shape-biased stream carrying V1, the stronger, texture-leaning stream extrastriate cortex — and tracks which images human observers resolve. The integrated behaviour is shape-biased because a behavioural rate is a property of a readout over components, not a report of any one of them; robustness, error consistency and dimensionality come apart from the rate (Fig. 5).

Finally we close the loop the paradox demands: the cue-conflict images themselves are pushed through the fitted encoding models, and the predicted cortical representations are asked whether they are texture-biased and whether they still carry the evidence the observers' image-by-image decisions follow (Fig. 6). They are not, and we report that failure; what survives is that the two bands carry opposite evidence in the same voxels.

## 2. Results

### 2.1 The model, and what the developmental trajectory costs

The network splits its input once, before any convolution (Fig. 1a): each image is converted to the DKL cone-opponent space, stream 1 receives the luminance channel and stream 2 the L−M channel, and each is a ResNet-18 trunk. Under the developmental diet the luminance channel passes through the acuity blur and contrast-sensitivity cut of Lu et al. (2026) at one age that rises linearly with the training step from birth to 300 months; every evaluation uses the endpoint, at which the magno stream sees nearly clean luminance (Methods 4.1). The trunks are initialised bit-identically, and a linear classifier reads out w·y₁ + (1−w)·y₂ of the two streams' penultimate features, with w = 0.5 during training, under one cross-entropy on that output and no loss on either stream.

The decisive control holds the diet at its 300-month endpoint from the first step: the same architecture, recipe, seeds and test input, without the developmental trajectory. The originating study's network (“BA network”) held a fixed mixture of ages instead (a σ = 4 px blur and a 48-month-old's contrast sensitivity) and is reported beside the model, with its no-diet arm; trained with our recipe, the same fixed diet separates the recipe from the trajectory. Networks were trained on MiniEcoset (112 classes; Thorat & Kietzmann, 2024) for 50 epochs, three seeds per condition (Methods 4.2).

The developmental trajectory costs accuracy, not stability (Fig. 1c): the model reaches 0.683 ± 0.002 validation top-1 at its final checkpoint, within 0.9 points of its best epoch in every seed, against 0.764 ± 0.003 for the control on the identical test input. It adds 1.8 points over the fixed BA diet under the same recipe (0.665 ± 0.003) and 0.067 of shape bias (training-time evaluation), and gives up 8.1 points against the network that learned the same input without the trajectory. A trajectory stopping at 48 months (one seed) is no better than the fixed diet (0.664 top-1, shape bias 0.637): the input has to reach adult acuity, not only start coarse. Every result for the model and its control below is from final checkpoints (Methods 4.7).

### 2.2 The two streams specialise in opposite directions, by different routes

Read out separately with a linear probe on the cue-conflict images, the two streams end on opposite sides of the shape–texture boundary: on exemplars the probe was not trained on, shape − texture accuracy is +0.316 for the magno stream and −0.156 for the parvo stream, every seed on its side (Fig. 2a; Methods 4.3), as in the BA network (+0.337 and −0.168) at 0.683 top-1 against its 0.445. Read out alone, the magno stream's cue-conflict shape bias is 0.850 and the parvo stream's 0.477, against 0.682 for the integrated readout (Table 1).

Both components are built by learning, and the input's trajectory decides the sign of one (Fig. 2a, Table 1). Untrained, the magno stream on the model's test input sits where undieted luminance does (+0.087 against +0.090 on held-out exemplars), and the parvo stream begins nearly unbiased (−0.033). Training on the maturing input adds +0.229 to the magno stream — nearly twice what training added in the BA network (+0.127), whose fixed blur put +0.210 into the input before any learning — and −0.123 to the parvo stream. Training on the same adult input from the first step drives the magno stream across the sign flip to −0.177, below its own parvo stream (−0.069): the magno stream's shape bias is a property not of the input it is tested on but of the input it learned from. The bias is present from the first evaluation, after one epoch at ages up to 6 months, and is kept as the input sharpens; whether the coarse-to-fine direction matters, or only exposure to coarse input, these runs cannot say. Nor is the stream a texture filter: on held-out exemplars it decodes texture at 0.148 (chance 0.0625) and shape at 0.464.

Which property of the input carries the effect? Crossing the carrier (achromatic luminance or chromatic L−M) with the developmental ramp gives four cells: achromatic with the ramp (+0.316), achromatic with the adult input from the start (−0.177), chromatic without the ramp (−0.156 in the model, −0.069 in the control) and chromatic with the ramp, the channel swap, which applies the same ramp to L−M with luminance untransformed (three seeds; −0.016). The ramp moves the achromatic stream by +0.49 and the chromatic stream by +0.05, leaving it below parity; the carrier × ramp interaction is +0.44 on held-out exemplars, and no seed of the conjunction cell (+0.30 to +0.33) overlaps a seed of any other cell. Only the ramp on an achromatic carrier crosses zero, as only the BA network's fixed transform on an achromatic carrier did (Supplementary Table S3). The swap reproduces its control on every other measure as well (0.760 top-1, an integrated rate of 0.389): applied to the chromatic channel, the trajectory buys neither the shape bias nor the accuracy cost. Blur-first training likewise helped a network's categorisation only with greyscale input (Jinsi et al., 2023).

The trajectory also decides which trunk carries the task. Read alone through readouts trained on the trunks' detached features (no gradient reaches the trunks; training-time evaluation), the model's luminance stream reaches 0.39 validation top-1 and its L−M stream 0.63, while in the control and the swap the luminance stream carries the task (0.75, against 0.25 and 0.17 for L−M); early in training the ramp all but silences the luminance trunk (0.07–0.09 at epoch 7, against 0.68–0.69 in the control and the swap). In each of the three networks the weaker trunk is the more shape-leaning one, but weakness does not cross parity: the swap's chromatic trunk is the weakest of all and stays below it (−0.016).

Two qualifications belong here. First, cue-conflict shape bias is computed only over the images a readout decides (426 to 580 of 1,200 in the model, 209 to 673 in the control; seed means); the probe, which classifies every image, and a forced two-way estimator preserve every ordering this paper draws a conclusion from, but the parvo stream sits within 0.03 of parity on both estimators, so its side is read from the probe. Second, the model is the less accurate network (0.683 against 0.764), but at the epoch where the control's top-1 matches the model's final 0.683 (epoch 7 of 50 in each of the three seeds) its magno stream's shape bias is 0.41–0.43, and at no epoch of any seed does it reach parity (highest 0.495; training-time evaluation).

### 2.3 In cortex, the magno stream carries V1 and the texture-biased stream the rest of the hierarchy

The thesis's representational similarity analysis (Kriegeskorte et al., 2008) could not separate a general stream factor from regional structure, so we fitted voxel-wise banded-ridge encoding models for each of the eight NSD subjects, one band per stream, partitioning each voxel's cross-validated explained variance into the part only the magno band accounts for (U_m) and the part only the parvo band does (U_p) (Methods 4.5); Fig. 3a plots the per-voxel index (U_m − U_p)/(U_m + U_p), median over reliable voxels per ROI, across V1v → hV4.

The profile crosses zero, and only with the maturing input. In the model the index runs from +0.55 in V1v, magno-explained, through zero at V2v to −0.76 in hV4, parvo-explained (Fig. 3a; in V1, U_m 0.10–0.11 against U_p 0.03–0.04 in 8/8 subjects in every seed). The control, on the identical test input, is magno-explained everywhere and nearly flat (+0.81 in V1v, +0.73 in hV4), as are the channel swap (+0.85, +0.83) and the BA no-diet network (+0.91, +0.83); the BA network is parvo-explained from V1 on and descends without crossing (−0.00 to −0.84).

Quantified as the contrast hV4 − mean(V1v, V1d) of the per-ROI median (Fig. 3b; Extended Data Table 1), the gradient is −1.259 to −1.291 across the three seeds against −0.062 to −0.086 for the control's, each seed against the control seed of the same index at the exact Wilcoxon floor for eight subjects (p = .0078, 8/8), with the same separation on the raw statistic U_m − U_p (−0.186 to −0.193 against −0.003 to −0.007). The channel swap, the same ramp on the chromatic carrier, has no gradient either (−0.019 normalised, +0.002 raw, seed means), and the model's lies below it in 8/8 subjects (p = .0078): in cortex as in behaviour, only the ramp on the achromatic carrier departs from the control.

The trajectory does not create the gradient; it decides what training does to the one the input supplies. An untrained network on the same test input already descends (−0.480 normalised, −0.195 raw, 8/8 subjects, p = .0078 on both). Training on the maturing input steepens it on the normalised index (to −1.27) and holds it on the raw difference (−0.19), so under the rule that a cortical claim needs both statistics (Methods 4.7) we claim only that it preserves the gradient; training on the adult input from the first step erases it on both (−0.070; −0.004, seed means).

Pushed through models fitted on natural scenes only, the cue-conflict images give a predicted magno − parvo shape-bias difference of +1.32 to +1.34 in the model against +0.07 for the control and −0.01 for the channel swap (8/8 subjects; Fig. 3d).

**The gradient continues past hV4 and does not turn.** On NSD's full atlases (Extended Data Fig. 4), relative to V1, hV4 sits at −1.12 and every extrastriate group lower — ventral (VO, PHC) −1.25, lateral (LO, TO) −1.27, dorsal (V3A/B, IPS0–2) −1.22 — each in 8/8 subjects (p = .0078), and no contrast among the three extrastriate groups departs from zero; beyond V3 the magno band contributes about 0.013 unique variance against 0.149 for the parvo band. Nor is the gradient an eccentricity or receptive-field-size contrast in disguise: it is negative within each of NSD's five eccentricity bands (−0.58 to −1.33) and each pRF-size tertile (−1.26 to −1.32), below the control's in every one (Extended Data Fig. 3). Nor does the estimator manufacture it: with a true magno − parvo difference of zero everywhere the recovered contrast is small and of the wrong sign (+0.018, p = .11), and corrected by each arm's own floor the model's gradient is −1.277 against −0.127 for the control (Supplementary Note S3).

Three things this section does not settle. First, the trajectory buys the gradient at a small cost in BOLD prediction against its control (noise-ceiling-normalised median r² 0.471 against 0.494, 8/8 subjects). Second, the gradient was not pre-specified: it is the survivor of seven regional hypotheses tested on the BA network, six of which failed, and is read as sign and ordering (Supplementary Notes S1 and S3). Third, “texture-biased” names the stream, not the reason cortex prefers it: in every network the stream carrying extrastriate cortex is also the trunk that carries the task (Results 2.2), and in the model it is the chromatic one too, so the design separates neither task load nor the carrier from texture bias; what only the model has is a V1 carried by the other trunk.

### 2.4 Behaviour reads out from both streams, and the rate is a property of the readout

The integrated readout's cue-conflict shape bias is 0.682 ± 0.020 (mean ± s.d. over three seeds; Table 1) against 0.374 for the control, 0.958 for the ten human observers and 0.172–0.429 for the CNNs of Geirhos et al. (2018a) on the identical images and statistic; integration costs 0.168 of the magno stream's 0.850 (Fig. 4c).

A readout of two oppositely biased components is nearly forced to land between them; what can be measured is how much of the rate it controls. Sweeping w while holding y₁ and y₂ exactly fixed (Fig. 4a) runs cue-conflict shape bias from 0.48 to 0.85 over 21 values of w and three seeds, and w = 0.5 reproduces each trained model exactly. The control's curve is flat (0.37–0.42), and so is the channel swap's (0.38–0.40): with both streams texture-leaning there is nothing for the weight to move. A two-layer head refitted on the frozen features at each w spans 106% of the frozen head's range (0.395 against 0.371) and 0.241 within 5 points of peak validation accuracy (Fig. 4b): the span belongs to the components, and its extremes are bought with accuracy.

**The readout weight cortex prefers descends the hierarchy.** With the readout's input z(w) = w·y₁ + (1 − w)·y₂ as the design of a voxelwise ridge encoding model, the w that maximises an ROI's median r² is the weight that region prefers (Methods 4.5; Extended Data Fig. 5; Extended Data Table 2). In the model that weight falls from 0.65 in V1 to 0.30–0.35 in every extrastriate group, below V1 in 8/8 subjects (Δ = −0.323 [−0.371, −0.269], exact p = .0078); in the control and the channel swap the gradient reverses (higher beyond hV4 in 8/8 subjects, and in 7/8 with one tie). The curves are shallow — every ROI is within 2% of its best r² at w = 0.5 — so this is an ordering of optima, not an r² difference, and the weight that best reproduces the observers' choices is not identified by these data (Supplementary Note S6).

This is the mechanism we propose for the paradox: a system holding a shape-biased and a texture-biased component, whose behaviour reads out from both, will look shape-biased behaviourally and texture-biased wherever the texture-biased component dominates — here beyond V1, with the shape-biased component present only if its input matured during training. It is an existence proof so far, not a measurement of the human readout; Results 2.5 tests it against the observers' own decisions.

### 2.5 Predicted cortex is not texture-biased on the cue-conflict images; in the same voxels, the two bands carry opposite evidence

Everything so far links cortex to the model on natural images and the model to the observers on the cue-conflict images; nothing links predicted cortex to the observers. So the 1,200 cue-conflict images were pushed through each subject's fitted encoders and each region's predicted pattern read against sixteen category templates from natural MiniEcoset photographs (the registered predictions, Methods 4.8); the fraction of images whose pattern is closer to the shape category's template than to the texture category's is the region's shape-bias rate.

The prediction fails at its first step (Fig. 6a). The predicted extrastriate representations are not texture-biased by this criterion: the model's rate is 0.53 in hV4, 0.47 in ventral and 0.50 in lateral cortex (8/8 subjects below 0.5 only in ventral), below the behavioural 0.68 everywhere but nowhere near texture dominance (P-C1 not met); the control and the swap sit lower still (0.39–0.51). By the plan's decision table the reading of record is `CORTEX_NOT_TEXTURE_BIASED_ON_THESE_IMAGES`, the same in the three registered sensitivity analyses.

The evidence is there, but not beyond the input floor (Fig. 6b). The shape margin tracks the observers' choices in every region (8/8 subjects positive in hV4, ventral and lateral cortex), above the permutation null in V1–hV4 but not at p < .01 beyond; and the floor carries 11.5% in hV4 and 13.0–13.8% beyond it, so the model exceeds it in 7/8 subjects in hV4 and in 0/8 and 1/8 beyond (P-C2 not met; the resolution target, P-C2b, likewise).

What does hold is the decomposition (Fig. 6c). In the same voxels of the same fit the luminance band's contribution is shape-biased in every region (0.66–0.73) and the L−M band's texture-biased (0.42–0.51), the joint fit between them, in 8/8 subjects and 24/24 seed-cells (P-C3 met): a sum of a shape-biased and a texture-biased component, as the model's layers are (Results 2.4), but not the texture-biased sum predicted beyond hV4.

The trajectory does not add to the correspondence (Fig. 6d). On the resolution target every model seed lies below every control and swap seed (U = 0, opposite to the prediction), and on the shape target the arms interleave (p = 1.0): the behavioural result of Fig. 5c is not recovered from predicted cortex (P-C4 not met). The profile (P-C5) rises from V1 to hV4 and falls beyond, the floor's is the reverse; Fig. 6e shows hV4 image by image.

### 2.6 Properties that do not follow from shape bias

If shape bias is a property of a readout over components, quantities the field treats as travelling with it need not, and over identical test inputs three do not (Fig. 5).

**Robustness.** The standard reading of Geirhos et al. (2018a, 2021) is that shape bias buys out-of-distribution robustness. The BA study pre-registered that prediction for its diet and it failed (Supplementary Table S5); the model repeats the failure against a control that sees the same test images (Methods 4.6; Fig. 5a; Extended Data Fig. 1). It is less robust than its control on four of the five corruption families neither was trained on (uniform noise −0.135, high-pass −0.136, false colour −0.084, rotation −0.040; every model seed below every control seed, exact Mann–Whitney p = 0.10, the floor for three seeds against three), within 0.01 on phase scrambling, and more robust only to low-pass (+0.078), a family its trajectory trains in: robustness appears where it was trained in and nowhere else, as when stylised training was decomposed (Mummadi et al., 2021).

**Agreement with human decisions.** Error consistency — Cohen's κ on trial-by-trial agreement — subtracts the agreement two decision-makers' marginal rates force (Geirhos et al., 2020); across the ten observers the human–human ceiling is κ = 0.234. It does not follow shape bias: along the readout sweep it rises with the rate only as far as the bound the two marginals place on κ (Geirhos et al., 2020; Klein et al., 2025; Fig. 5b; Supplementary Note S6), and the BA study's pre-registered prediction, that the diet would make the network more human-consistent than its control, is not supported (13.2% against 11.9% of the ceiling, seeds interleaved, exact Mann–Whitney p = 1.0). The measure does detect a correspondence when one is there, and here the trajectory supplies it. Asked not which cue the observers pick on an image but whether it resolves to either cue at all, the model's readout recovers 22.3% of the split-half ceiling on held-out images (seeds 19.6–24.3%), against 9.0% for the control (6.6–10.4%) and 8.2% for the channel swap (7.1–10.4%), every model seed above every seed of both (exact Mann–Whitney p = 0.10, the floor); on which cue is picked the three are interleaved (13.3%, 10.4% and 9.5%; p ≥ 0.70; Fig. 5c). Unlike the BA network's version of this contrast, which crossing weights and test input removed (Fig. 5d), it belongs to the trajectory: the model and its control share their test input.

**Representational dimensionality.** The magno/parvo ratio of the streams' participation ratios on NSD images ends above parity in every subject and seed of the model (24/24; seed means 1.88–1.98) and below it in 23 of its control's 24 (seed means 0.89–0.95), from the same untrained starting point (1.63) and the same test input (Extended Data Fig. 2). The channel swap shows that dimensionality is not shape bias: it also ends above parity (24/24; seed means 1.27–2.15) with neither stream shape-biased, because its ramped chromatic stream contracts (participation ratio 52–86 against 116–124 in the control) where the model's ramped luminance stream expands (272–281).

## 3. Discussion

**The two questions.** Can two streams fed magno- and parvocellular-like inputs diverge under one objective while behaviour is shape-biased, and does such a system bear on why the ventral stream can be texture-like while behaviour is not? To the first the answer is yes, and the route is specific: both components are built by learning, and the luminance stream learns a shape bias only when its own input matures during training. The same adult input from the first step yields two texture-leaning streams, and the same ramp on the L−M channel leaves the network where its control is in behaviour, cortex and accuracy. To the second it is an existence proof, and it needs the same conjunction: only the network whose luminance input matured divides the cortical hierarchy, its shape-biased stream carrying V1 and its stronger, texture-leaning stream extrastriate cortex, and only it tracks which images the observers resolve (22.3% of the split-half ceiling, against 9.0% and 8.2% on identical input), while its behaviour, reading out from both, is shape-biased. Read as claims about one representation the two conflict; read as claims about a readout over components, they do not.

**Where the account fails.** The registered cortex-to-decision test (Results 2.5) asked predicted cortex to show the paradox itself: extrastriate representations texture-biased by the cue-conflict criterion that still track the cue the observers chose. It does not: the predicted representations are not texture-biased on these images (rates 0.47–0.53 beyond V3), their correspondence beyond hV4 is matched by the inputs' oriented energy, the trajectory adds nothing, and only the decomposition survives. The readout account keeps its behavioural half and its cortical correspondence on natural images, not the claim that the texture-dominated cortical image of a cue-conflict stimulus carries the shape evidence; whether that is a fact about extrastriate cortex or about reading predicted responses to stylised images against natural templates, the data do not separate.

**What the readout account licenses.** A model matching the human shape-bias rate is routinely described as having human-like representations; here that inference fails twice. The same network holds components at +0.32 and −0.16 on held-out exemplars and behaves at 0.682, and one scalar over frozen representations moves the rate across most of the range the field's models occupy, so the rate does not identify a representation; and trial-by-trial agreement with the observers never exceeds 29% of the human–human ceiling in our networks, so the rate is at best a weak proxy for the decision process. Prior work established that a representation can hold more shape than its behaviour reveals (Hermann et al., 2020; Islam et al., 2021) and that the measured rate depends on the test design (Tartaglini et al., 2022), on a prompt (Gavrikov et al., 2024) and on a network's spatial-frequency channel (Subramanian et al., 2023), and retraining only the last layer reduces ImageNet networks' reliance on texture (Izmailov et al., 2022; Kirichenko et al., 2023); new here is one scalar over fixed components, the head untouched, its cortical grounding, and the test of the account where it has to hold — on the observers' own decisions, against the predicted cortical representations of the same images — which it fails (Results 2.5).

**What a developmental trajectory does, and what it costs.** The intuitive account of low-pass developmental input is informational: coarse input shields the learner from texture, so it learns shape. Our results support a version of it in which the input's history matters and the information at test does not: the model and its control see the same images at test, and what differs is whether the magno stream's early learning happened on coarse, low-contrast input (cf. critical periods in deep networks; Achille et al., 2019). Its route runs through the competition between the trunks: the coarse early input hands the task to the chromatic trunk, and the luminance trunk, learning beside it from a world that sharpens, ends the weaker and shape-biased; the swap shows that being the weaker trunk is not enough (Results 2.2). The BA network's fixed 48-month transform instead put most of its magno stream's shape bias into the input (+0.210 of +0.337 before training), at a cost in accuracy (0.556) and BOLD prediction (0.395); the trajectory costs less (8.1 points of top-1 against its control, robustness to corruptions it was not trained on, 0.024 of noise-ceiling-normalised r²). Its interest is as a model of development, and its prediction is developmental: a visual system whose early input is coarse should show a stream-level shape bias that a system given adult input from the start, on the same images, does not.

**Limitations.** The load-bearing cortical result rests on eight subjects from one dataset and is read as sign and ordering only. The mapping between our chromatic stream and texture-selective cortex is by explanatory role, not content, and in every network the trunk that carries extrastriate cortex also carries the task, so the cortical result cannot separate texture bias from task load; trunks matched in accuracy would. One trajectory shape was tested, and whether its direction matters is open: the control removes both the coarse-to-fine order and the exposure to coarse input, and a reversed or age-shuffled schedule would separate them. No single-stream network was trained with the trajectory. The model of record was changed after its first seed was seen; the rule for the change, and the control that could have blocked it, were fixed before seeds 1–2 and the control were trained (Methods 4.2). Four predictions were committed before their analyses ran on the BA network, and every outcome, failures included, is reported (Supplementary Note S5); on the model, the robustness prediction fails again and the error-consistency one is not supported. The cortex-to-decision test (Results 2.5) reads predicted, not measured, responses to stylised images outside the encoders' training distribution, against templates from natural photographs; it was registered before its runs were submitted (Supplementary Note S5), its reading of record is a failure, and a defect in its permutation null on the shape target, found after the run, is disclosed and corrected in Methods 4.8. Claims deliberately not made are listed in Supplementary Note S1, and the limitations in full in Supplementary Note S6.

## 4. Methods

### 4.1 Front end

RGB images are converted to the DKL cone-opponent space through RGB→XYZ (D65)→LMS (Hunt–Pointer–Estévez)→DKL at 256 × 256 px, each channel normalised to [0, 1] with the training set's global range and quantised to 8 bits exactly as the training data were stored (Derrington et al., 1984). Stream 1 receives the luminance channel and stream 2 the L−M channel. Under the developmental diet the luminance channel passes through the acuity and contrast stages of the developmental visual diet of Lu et al. (2026) (colour stage off, the channel being achromatic), both evaluated at one age a: the acuity stage is a Gaussian blur whose σ follows the diet's acuity curve at a, scaled to the 256-px image (0.50 px at 48 months; inert, kernel size 1, beyond about six years), and the contrast stage is the scale-free contrast-sensitivity cut evaluated at a (`cs_logspan_start` = 5 × 10⁻³, decomposition mode “keep”; `src/analysis/encoding/dkl_frontend.py`, `apply_dvd_diet`). During training a rises linearly with the optimisation step from 0 to 300 months (6 months per epoch), as the diet's own age curve does; every evaluation — validation, cue-conflict, probes, NSD images and corruptions — uses a = 300 months. The conditions are: `fs2_dvd300` (model of record: luminance + ramp | L−M); `fs2_dvdfix300` (control: luminance + the 300-month diet from the first step | L−M), identical to the model at test; `fs2_dvd300swap` (luminance | L−M + ramp; training); `fs2_dvd48` (the ramp stopped at 48 months; one seed); and `fs2_bcs` (the BA network's fixed diet under the recipe of Methods 4.2). The BA network `dkl_bcs` (luminance + isotropic Gaussian blur, σ = 4 px, kernel 33, reflect padding + the same contrast cut at a fixed 48 months | L−M), its no-diet arm `dkl_base` and its two front-end controls `dkl_bcs_swap` and `dkl_bcs_parvo_highsf` are those of the originating study, retrained at three seeds with its recipe (PAPER_v2, Methods 4.1). Every analysis pipeline reads the front end from the checkpoint it evaluates and refuses a mismatch (`check_frontend`). Evaluation stimuli pass through the identical front end.

### 4.2 Architecture and training

Two ResNet-18 trunks (He et al., 2016), one per stream, initialised bit-identically, with a joint linear classification head over the weighted average of their penultimate features (w = 0.5), trained with one cross-entropy on the joint output (label smoothing 0.1) and no loss on either stream. Two per-stream heads are trained alongside on detached stream features to log each stream's accuracy during training; they send no gradient into the trunks (checked: zero gradient leak), and no measure in this paper uses them — the streams alone are read through the joint head (Methods 4.3). MiniEcoset at 256 px, 112 classes (Thorat & Kietzmann, 2024). 50 epochs, batch 256, AdamW (lr 10⁻³, weight decay 0.05, none on biases and normalisation parameters; Loshchilov & Hutter, 2019), two warm-up epochs then cosine decay to zero, an exponential moving average of the weights (decay 0.9995) that is evaluated and saved as the final checkpoint, bf16 autocast, and the augmentation set of Lu et al. (2026) (horizontal flip, rotation ±15°, colour jitter, random equalise, perspective, sharpness; random grayscale for RGB inputs only). Three seeds per condition. The recipe was fixed on the BA network's fixed diet before any trajectory was trained — eleven single-objective variants of that network (readout weight 0.5–0.9, label smoothing, gradient modulation, 25 or 50 epochs, norm-pinned features) all reached 0.64–0.67 top-1, and `fs2_bcs` was the best — and is unchanged for the trajectory and its controls. The model's seeds and the control's seed 0 trained on one NVIDIA B200 each (about 4.3 h per run), the control's seeds 1–2 and the swap on one RTX A5000 each (18.3–21.3 h per run; 99.9 GPU-hours for the five), with the same code and data. The rule for replacing `fs2_bcs` as the model of record — the trajectory's three-seed mean must exceed it on top-1 and on shape bias, and the adult-from-the-start control must fall short on shape bias, or the gain would be the adult input at test rather than the trajectory — was written after the trajectory's first seed and before seeds 1–2 and the control were trained; it passed on the training-time evaluation (+1.8 points, +0.067 shape bias; control −0.32 shape bias). The BA network's runs keep the thesis's recipe: 150 epochs, batch 256, Adam (lr 1 × 10⁻⁴, β₁ = 0.9; Kingma & Ba, 2017), cross-entropy, the same augmentations, weights saved every 25 epochs; their compute accounting is that of PAPER_v2 (584 GPU-hours on RTX A5000s for 21 runs; 930 kWh and 325 kg CO₂e as an upper bound for the whole of that study). No hyperparameter search was run for any trajectory arm.

### 4.3 Behaviour and probe

Cue-conflict shape bias is computed on the Geirhos et al. (2018a) style-transfer set restricted to the 16 categories present in MiniEcoset and to images whose shape and texture categories differ (1,200 images). Logits are restricted to the 16 columns; the argmax is scored as a shape or texture response; images decided as neither are excluded from the denominator and their count is reported. Bootstrap 95% confidence intervals are over images. A second, forced two-way estimator keeps every image and scores whether the logit of the image's shape category exceeds that of its texture category; it is reported beside the primary estimator and computed from the same stored logits. The readout is full = classifier(w·y₁ + (1−w)·y₂), magno_only = classifier(w·y₁), parvo_only = classifier((1−w)·y₂), all through the same classifier and bias term; the w sweep evaluates full at 21 values of w on stored features. The refitted heads (Linear(512, 112) and Linear(512, 512)→ReLU→Linear(512, 112)) were trained on w·F₁ + (1−w)·F₂ of MiniEcoset training features at each w with an identical budget (Adam, lr 10⁻³, batch 1024, 20 epochs) and the same initialisation seed at every w; the span within 5 points of peak validation accuracy is taken over the w at which the refitted head's validation top-1 is within 0.05 of its best (`tmp_analysis/nonlinear_readout_summary.py`). Human observers are the ten of the same release (1,280 trials each), scored by the identical rule; our recomputation reproduces the published 0.958 and the published AlexNet, GoogLeNet and VGG-16 values. Error consistency is Cohen's κ (Cohen, 1960) on the binary shape/texture choice over images both parties decided, with expected agreement p_m·p_h + (1−p_m)(1−p_h), reported as a percentage of the human–human κ over all 45 observer pairs (Geirhos et al., 2020). Per-image correspondence uses the observers' per-image shape rate and cue-resolution rate (split-half reliabilities r = .624 and .728 after category removal; .658 and .794 before), the models' shape-minus-texture logit margin, w chosen on half the images and scored on the held-out half, as a percentage of each target's own ceiling. The linear probe reduces each stream's features to 256 PCA components and fits multinomial logistic regression (C = 1.0) under 5-fold stratified cross-validation to shape and texture labels separately; bias is shape accuracy minus texture accuracy on the same folds. Because the 1,200 images are built from 160 object and 48 texture exemplars, stratified folds leave every test image's exemplars in the training folds, so the primary probe is exemplar-balanced: three exemplars per category for both labels (three of each shape category's ten, drawn at random; the texture set has three), texture images subsampled to the same per-category count, scored leave one exemplar out with PCA (min(256, n_train − 1) components) and scaling fitted inside each fold, mean of 20 draws with the 5–95% interval over draws (`src/analysis/generality/exemplar.py`; `docs/PLAN_EXEMPLAR_2026-09-21.md`). The stratified-fold probe is reported beside it as exemplar-shared. With about 250 training images per fold the balanced probe is sensitive to preprocessing (PCA fitted on all images and then standardised compresses every bias toward zero, whereas fewer components or no standardisation agree in sign with the primary estimator); the primary preprocessing is fixed as stated, and the spread over the five held-out variants is reported beside it (`tmp_analysis/figs_v2/exemplar_probe_spread.py`).

### 4.4 Effective dimensionality

Participation ratio (Σλ)²/Σλ² of the stream feature matrix on 1,000 NSD images per subject (Gao et al., 2017), computed Gram-side, reported as the magno/parvo ratio with parity at 1.0. Subjects are the replication unit within a seed; seeds are reported separately and never pooled. For the model and its control the stream features are those cached for the encoding fits (`tmp_analysis/fs2_pr_ratio.py`, `results/figures_v2/fs2_pr_ratio.json`), and the reference is the untrained network on the same front end; for the BA network the learning-free reference is a Gabor oriented-energy bank applied to each stream's own transformed input.

### 4.5 Encoding

NSD (Allen et al., 2022) `betas_fithrf_GLMdenoise_RR` single-trial betas, converted to percent signal change and z-scored per session per voxel, `nsdgeneral` mask, eight subjects. Splits are by 73k image id with the shared-515 images held out. Stream features for each subject's stimuli are reduced by PCA to 1,000 components per stream (fit on training images only) and entered as two bands into a banded ridge regression (himalaya; Dupré la Tour et al., 2022) with 3-fold GroupKFold by image id, per-voxel alphas and per-voxel band weights. Unique variance U_m (U_p) is the drop in cross-validated r² when the magno (parvo) band is removed. Noise ceilings are NSD's `ncsnr`; voxels below 0.1 are excluded and any ROI cell with fewer than 20 surviving voxels is reported as missing. ROIs are the `prf-visualrois` labels of the Wang et al. (2015) atlas as distributed with NSD (V1v, V1d, V2v, V2d, V3v, V3d, hV4), with hierarchical rank [1, 1, 2, 2, 3, 3, 4]. The model, its control and the untrained network on its front end are fitted with the identical pipeline, reading each checkpoint's front end from the checkpoint (Methods 4.1). In the BA network the canonical-correlation spectrum between the two bands runs from ≈0 to 0.93, so each band retains directions the other cannot predict and unique variance is identifiable; a recovery simulation with the fitted feature geometry and a known across-ROI split returns the true ordering exactly (ρ = +1.000 in 8/8 subjects, both arms), which is why the gradient is reported as sign and ordering rather than as calibrated magnitude. The BA network's swap and `parvo_highsf` arms are fitted for all three seeds, as its diet and no-diet arms are, and their behavioural and probe measures use the same three seeds (Supplementary Table S3). Both are degenerate by construction and in every seed — the swapped network leaves the parvo band a median unique variance 0.005–0.010 of the magno band's, the parvo + high-SF network leaves the magno band 0.003–0.005 of the parvo band's — so they enter no cortical claim beyond the sign and size of their unique variances (PAPER_v2 Extended Data Table 1; Supplementary Table S10). The fits of this section were originally submitted at about 160 GB of memory per subject, which is not the size of the data: the peak is the group-ridge random search, which forms an (n_alphas × n_test × n_train) array per gamma sample and fold, about 30 GB in float64 with all 21 alphas at once. Batching that search seven alphas at a time changes no estimate — a refit of the diet seed-0 subject-01 fit reproduces the stored one at voxelwise r = 1.00000 on both unique variances, with the largest per-ROI index difference below 10⁻⁴ — and the same fits then peak at 32.6 GB (`slurm/encode_equiv_probe.sh`, `slurm/encode_cc_controls.sh`). Eccentricity bands are NSD's `prf-eccrois` labels (0–0.5°, 0.5–1°, 1–2°, 2–4°, > 4°); continuous pRF eccentricity, size and R² are NSD's population-receptive-field estimates in the same functional space, and the within-subject regressions of Supplementary Table S8 use z-scored log(eccentricity + 0.5) and log(size) over voxels with pRF R² > 5%. The per-voxel predicted shape-bias index is obtained by pushing the cue-conflict set through each fitted encoding model. For Extended Data Fig. 4 and Supplementary Table S10 the same voxelwise fits were relabelled, without refitting, with the full Kastner2015 atlas as distributed with NSD (adding VO1/2, PHC1/2, LO1/2, TO1/2, V3A/B and IPS0–2; IPS3–5, SPL1 and FEF have no voxels inside `nsdgeneral`), with NSD's `streams` parcellation and with the fLoc face, place, body and word ROIs; regional means are unweighted means of the ROI medians, and region contrasts are tested per subject with the exact Wilcoxon test. For the cortex-optimal readout weight (Extended Data Fig. 5; Extended Data Table 2) the classifier's own input z(w) = w·y₁ + (1 − w)·y₂, where y₁ and y₂ are the 512-dimensional stream outputs the classifier mixes, was the design of a single-band ridge model of every `nsdgeneral` voxel for w ∈ {0, 0.05, …, 1}, with the same split, grouped folds and alpha grid as the banded model and columns z-scored on the training trials; because z(w) sums the streams dimension by dimension the ridge cannot undo the mixture, so w is a constraint on the design rather than a re-parameterisation of the two-band model. w*_cortex is the w that maximises the median test r² over an ROI's reliable voxels (seeds averaged per subject); the 1,024-dimensional concatenation [y₁, y₂] is fitted as a reference and not as a bound, because although every mixture is a linear map of it the two models are regularised separately on a shared alpha grid, and in the model and its control the best mixture exceeds the fitted concatenation in 15 of the 112 subject × ROI × arm cells by a median 0.0023 r² — more than the 0.0012 the sweep itself buys from w = ½ to w*, so w*_cortex is read as the location of an optimum and never as an r² difference (Extended Data Table 2); and the behaviour-optimal weight is the trial-fit w of Fig. 4 (Methods 4.3) for each of the ten observers, reported with its per-fit bootstrap interval and permutation p and not compared with w*_cortex, because it is not identified (Results 2.4). The koniocellular control substitutes S−(L+M) for L−M in stream 2 of the untrained network at each of three seeds, each against the L−M network of its own seed, contrast-matched to L−M per image, with the magno band bit-identical across arms (gate max|Δr²_magno| < 10⁻⁴, passed at 0.000).

### 4.6 Out-of-distribution robustness

Corruptions are applied to RGB before the front end and scored in the native 112-class label space. Five families were declared untrained-on before the BA study's run (uniform noise, high-pass, phase scrambling, rotation, false colour) and two as trained-in positive controls (low-pass, contrast); the declaration is kept for the model, whose trajectory trains in both low-pass and contrast. Reported as the area under the relative-accuracy curve across severities (accuracy over clean accuracy), at final checkpoints for the model and its control and at each seed's best saved checkpoint for the BA network. Two implementation differences from Lu et al. (2026) remain: our diet is a transform on one stream's input of a two-stream network rather than an augmentation on a single trunk, and we train on MiniEcoset and score corruptions in that label space, applied before the front end. The third difference of the BA network, a fixed rather than scheduled age, does not apply to the model.

### 4.7 Statistics

Within-subject comparisons use the paired exact Wilcoxon signed-rank test, n = 8 (exact two-sided floor p = .0078); seed-level comparisons use the exact Mann–Whitney U, 3 v 3 (floor p = 0.100, reached only on complete separation), and seeds are never pooled with subjects. Every cortical contrast is computed both on the raw per-voxel difference U_m − U_p and on the normalised index (U_m − U_p)/(U_m + U_p), each summarised by its median over reliable voxels per ROI, and a claim is made only where the two agree; disagreements are reported as unresolved. Equivalence uses two one-sided tests with bounds fixed before any interval was computed (Schuirmann, 1987; Lakens, 2017). No multiple-comparison correction is claimed: with an exact floor of .0078, Holm–Bonferroni over any family larger than six cannot yield a rejection, so claims rest on complete separation, agreement of both statistics, replication across seeds and effect size (dz = −14.3 for the model's gradient, seed means per subject; −6.9 for the BA network's and −0.59 for the control's by the same computation). The model and its control are evaluated at their final checkpoints throughout, which for this recipe are the exponential moving average of the weights and lie within 0.9 points of top-1 of the best epoch; the BA network's recipe declines late (final 0.445 against best 0.556), so its behavioural and robustness results use each seed's best saved checkpoint where stated, as in PAPER_v2, and its cortical fits the final checkpoint. Four predictions — the front-end 2×2, the inverted-front-end prediction, the corruption families and the error-consistency hypothesis — were written into a version-controlled plan and committed before the corresponding analysis was run on the BA network (internal pre-specification, not registry-based); two failed and are reported as failures. They were not re-registered for the model; its replacement rule (Methods 4.2) is the one decision fixed before its seeds 1–2 and control were trained.

### 4.8 Predicted cortical evidence on the cue-conflict images

Registered in `docs/PLAN_CORTEX_DECISION_2026-10-26.md` before the runs were submitted; every definition below is the plan's. The encoding models of Methods 4.5 are refit from the cached stream features by the identical code path and seed for the model, its control and the channel swap (three seeds each), for the untrained network on the model's front end, and for an input-level floor (the same banded ridge on oriented-energy features of the two streams' transformed inputs), this time storing the predicted response of every `nsdgeneral` voxel to three further stimulus sets, projected through the PCA and the band statistics of each subject's training split exactly as the cue-conflict design of Supplementary Table S2 was: the 1,200 cue-conflict images of Methods 4.3, the 80 same-cue images of the same release (shape category equal to texture category) and 800 templates, the first 50 MiniEcoset validation images of each of the 16 Geirhos categories. A refit must reproduce its stored fit (largest absolute difference of test r² and of either unique variance over reliable voxels ≤ 10⁻³) or it is excluded and listed. Regions are V1–hV4 of `prf-visualrois` and the ventral, lateral and parietal divisions of NSD's `streams`, reliable voxels only, a cell with fewer than 20 voxels missing. Within a region, voxels are centred and scaled by the templates' mean and s.d.; the template of a category is the mean normalised pattern of its 50 images; the evidence E_ic is the Fisher-z Pearson correlation over voxels between image i's predicted pattern and template c. The shape margin is E_i,shape − E_i,texture, the cue margin max(E_i,shape, E_i,texture) minus the largest of the other fourteen, and the cortical shape-bias rate the fraction of images with a positive shape margin (bootstrap interval over images). Correspondence with the observers is the Pearson correlation across images between the shape margin and the per-image shape rate, and between the cue margin and the per-image resolution rate, both sides residualised on shape- and texture-category main effects (Methods 4.3), as a percentage of the respective split-half ceiling (.624 and .728); its null permutes the observers' profile within shape × texture cells (2,000 permutations, one sequence for every cell), and its interval is a cluster bootstrap over the 160 object exemplars. Seeds are averaged per subject at the level of per-image margins, and the joint, magno-only and parvo-only estimators of the same fit are read separately. The same-cue images are the manipulation check: nearest-template accuracy against chance 1/16 (exact binomial, p < .01), failing which a region is reported as not estimable. Tests are the exact Wilcoxon signed-rank over subjects and the exact Mann–Whitney over seed means (Methods 4.7). Sensitivity analyses fixed in the plan: Spearman correlation over voxels, V1 as the early division of `streams`, and all voxels without the reliability mask. `src/analysis/encoding/cortex_decision.py`; `tmp_analysis/test_cortex_decision.py`.

**Predictions.** Two things are asked of each region. First, whether its predicted representation is texture-biased on these images: the account predicts a rate below 0.5 in hV4, ventral and lateral cortex in every subject (P-C1), where the behavioural readout of the same network stands at 0.68. Second, whether that texture-biased representation nevertheless carries the evidence the observers use: across images, the shape margin is correlated with the observers' per-image shape rate after shape- and texture-category main effects are removed, as a percentage of the split-half ceiling (.62), against an input-level floor (the same encoders on oriented-energy features of the two streams' inputs) and a permutation null that preserves the category structure (P-C2), and the cue margin likewise with the per-image resolution rate (ceiling .73). The band decomposition says which stream carries each sign in the same voxels (P-C3); the control and the channel swap, on identical input, say whether the trajectory is needed (P-C4); the regional profile is reported with no prediction (P-C5).

**Defect found after the run.** Six of the 1,200 images have no resolved observer trial, so the per-image shape rate is undefined there; the observed correspondence masks them, but the registered code's permutation null and exemplar bootstrap for the shape target did not, so the analysis of record (`results/cortex_decision/ctd_analysis.json`) reports a vacuous p = .0005 and an undefined interval for every shape-target correspondence. The resolution target has no such image and is unaffected, as are the rates, the correspondences themselves, the subject counts, the input-floor comparison and the reading of record. The null and intervals quoted in Results 2.5 and Fig. 6 for the shape target are recomputed with those images masked per permutation, same permutation sequence and bootstrap seed (`tmp_analysis/ctd_h_null_fix.py`, `results/cortex_decision/ctd_h_null_fix.json`); the module is corrected, with a test, for future runs, and a full rerun with the corrected module on the same refits (`slurm/ctd_analysis_v2.sh`; `results/cortex_decision/v2/`) reproduces the analysis of record in every other quantity and the corrected null and intervals exactly (`tmp_analysis/ctd_compare_v2.py`), with the same reading in the main and the three sensitivity runs. Fig. 6e's per-image margins are recomputed from the stored predictions by `tmp_analysis/ctd_perimage_margins.py`.

## Ethics and human participants

This study collected no new human data. Both human datasets are existing, de-identified public releases analysed under their own terms. NSD was collected under the approval of the University of Minnesota institutional review board with written informed consent from all eight participants (Allen et al., 2022); the behavioural data were collected under the approval described in Geirhos et al. (2018a). Secondary analysis of two de-identified public datasets required no additional approval under our institution's policy, and no participant-identifying information was accessed.

**Sex and demographics.** Participant sex was not under our control and is not a design factor; no analysis is disaggregated by sex, and at eight participants and ten observers none could be, because the exact tests used already sit at their resolution floor on the full samples. We do not restate sex or age: the Geirhos per-observer files carry only trial-level fields, so no per-observer demographic exists in the data we analysed, and for NSD we have not verified values against the released subject-information file and therefore point to the NSD release rather than transcribe from prose.

**Constraints on generality.** The neuroimaging sample is eight adults with normal or corrected-to-normal vision in the United States; the behavioural reference is ten adults tested in a German university laboratory. Both are small, Western, convenience-recruited adult samples. Claims about “human observers” should be read as claims about these samples; nothing here establishes cross-cultural, developmental or clinical generality, and the developmental motivation of the model's front end is a motivation for the model, not a claim about any developmental population we measured.

## Supplementary Note S1 — claims deliberately not made

- That the two-stream split is necessary for the dissociation: whether frozen single-trunk networks show the same readout property is not examined in this paper (the analyses that asked it are reported outside it; Supplementary Note S5).
- That the two-stream split causes a shape-bias advantage over a single stream: at matched capacity the arms do not separate at n = 3, and capacity alone moves the rate by a comparable amount (Results 2.4).
- Any dorsal/ventral stream mapping. The thesis's framing “magno→dorsal, parvo→ventral” was tested rather than dropped: the pre-declared dorsal−ventral interaction is +0.0245 on the diet model and +0.0122 (p = .195) without the low-pass front end, so it does not survive its own control. Read directly on the Wang atlas, dorsal (V3A/B, IPS0–2) and ventral (VO1/2, PHC1/2) regions are indistinguishable under both arms (dorsal − ventral +0.015 with the diet, +0.005 without, p > .1; Extended Data Fig. 4c): the diet's magno stream contributes no unique variance anywhere beyond V3.
- hMT+ specificity: at n = 8 the two statistics disagree (p = .195 normalised, .039 raw) and the effect is dead in the blur control.
- A foveal/peripheral headline: the foveal band's sign flips when the blur is removed (−0.057 → +0.051), so it is a front-end artefact.
- That the chromatic stream predicts colour-biased cortex: pooled VO is +0.0045 (p = .46), bounded within ±0.017; hV4's parvo preference is weak (−0.012, 2/8). Substituting the koniocellular axis makes the prediction worse, and worse specifically in the colour set (hV4 −0.029, VO −0.024, each p = .0078, 0/8).
- Where in cortex the diet's shape-bias effect lands: a diet-specific hV4/LO step does not survive the full control set.
- That integration produces human-like shape bias, or that the diet improves neural predictivity: it does neither.
- That the diet makes the model's image-level difficulty profile more human-like: the crossed 2×2 shows train/test matching, not a weights effect.
- That human cortex uses this mechanism: a single-stream account in which texture-like ventral representations feed shape-biased behaviour directly is not excluded by these data.

Why the three regional claims above failed together, and what it implies for the survivor: without the low-pass front end essentially every ROI is magno-preferring, so the blur manufactures apparent regional specificity by handicapping the magno stream until the parvo stream can win somewhere — the same general-factor pathology that defeated the thesis's RSA, reappearing in encoding form. Any regional claim on these models must be shown to survive removal of the blur, and the ventral gradient is reported because it does.

Second, stratifying the same voxels by pRF size gives the same result within size tertiles (−0.71, −0.77 and −0.90 under the diet against −0.08 in each tertile without it). A learning-free Gabor bank on the diet front end carries no such structure (+0.035, p = .15), so it is not a property of the input filter alone; an untrained network carries it on either front end, however (diet front end +0.083, 7/8, p = .016 on the eccentricity partial and −0.038, 6/8, p = .039 on the size partial; undieted front end +0.089, 7/8, p = .023 and −0.038, 8/8, p = .0078), and training with the diet leaves it where random weights put it — trained minus untrained is +0.036 (6/8, p = .11) on the normalised statistic and −0.077 (2/8, p = .11) on the raw one, opposite in sign and neither significant — while the network trained without the diet carries none of it on either statistic (+0.028 raw, −0.004 normalised; the paired decrease from the untrained network is significant on the raw statistic only, −0.374, 0/8, p = .0078, against −0.087, 2/8, p = .15). The diet preserves a dissociation that random convolutional weights already produce; it neither installs nor amplifies it (Supplementary Table S8). Within the diet arm, then, the ventral gradient and the pRF-size dependence are the same fact seen on two axes — larger receptive fields, further along the hierarchy, are increasingly explained by the texture-biased stream — and the network trained without the diet shows neither.

**The estimator's own bias points the wrong way.** The mean noise ceiling declines from V1v to hV4 (ρ = −0.89 against rank), a nuisance falling along the axis of the effect, so we simulated the instrument: synthetic voxels from each arm's fitted feature spaces, the measured noise ceilings imposed, the true magno − parvo difference set to zero everywhere. The recovered contrast is a small, positive floor (+0.038 with the diet's feature geometry, +0.051 without, 8/8 subjects each), flat across ROIs (+0.025 to +0.027 per ROI), and indistinguishable from zero on the raw statistic — opposite in sign to the observed effect and without its gradient. Because the floor is a signed bias, the right comparison is a paired displacement: the real contrast minus each subject's own floor is −0.871 with the diet and −0.130 without, each non-zero in every subject, the diet arm's 6.7 times larger (Supplementary Table S7). The floor subtraction relocates the recovery simulation's residual misfit rather than removing it, and we do not correct that residual, because the correction is under-specified: six variants swing the no-diet arm between 4/8 and 6/8 subjects and between p = .055 and p = 1.00, while the diet arm is non-zero in all eight subjects at p = .0078 under all six (Supplementary Table S7). The no-diet arm's non-zero gradient therefore rests on the floor correction alone, and since the simulation is not magnitude-faithful (it recovers the true contrast with a slope of 1.21 to 1.28), we read −0.834 as a sign-and-ordering result, not a calibrated effect size. The raw statistic per ROI is drawn beside the normalised one in Extended Data Fig. 3a; the diet arm's normalised index approaches −0.84 at hV4, near the index's bound, and the raw profile carries the same ordering without that bound.

## Supplementary Note S4 — passages condensed in the main text

The main text was shortened to the journal's length. Every paragraph it condensed is given here in its full, pre-condensation wording, under the section it came from, so no number, control or failed prediction was dropped. These passages are PAPER_v2's and describe the BA network: “the diet” in them is its fixed 48-month transform under the thesis's recipe, not the model's trajectory. Where they differ from the main text, the main text is the current statement. Tables are not repeated.

**Introduction.**

Humans presented with an image whose shape and texture point to different categories name the shape: on the cue-conflict images of Geirhos et al. (2018a), ten observers did so on 96% of decided trials, while ImageNet-trained convolutional networks named the texture. Closing that gap has become a standard target for biologically motivated models (Hermann et al., 2020; Jang & Tong, 2021), and a developmental visual diet — training on input whose acuity, contrast sensitivity and chromatic sensitivity follow the human developmental trajectory — is among the most effective routes to it (Lu et al., 2026).

A second body of evidence sits uneasily beside the first: along the ventral stream, object representations are well described by texture statistics — category-selective human cortex fails to discriminate natural images from texture-matched scrambles yet still discriminates categories (Jagadeesh & Gardner, 2022), macaque V4 responses to natural textures are predicted by their image statistics (Okazawa et al., 2015), and in inferotemporal cortex texture is the more decodable cue (Jagadeesh & Livingstone, 2024; Komatsu & Goda, 2018). The premise is scoped — one human study with one manipulation, the rest macaque, and “texture-like” meaning that a texture-statistics description suffices, not that shape is absent — and the argument needs only that the ventral description is substantially texture-like.

If behaviour is shape-biased and the ventral stream texture-biased, either the two measures disagree about what shape bias is, or behaviour is not a readout of one representation. The second horn is a hypothesis about architecture that single-trunk models of the ventral stream cannot express, and two facts make it concrete: the visual system receives not one image but a luminance-dominated, spatially coarse magnocellular signal and a chromatically opponent, finer parvocellular one, separated before cortex and later recombined (Livingstone & Hubel, 1987; Kaplan & Shapley, 1982; Derrington et al., 1984; Nassi & Callaway, 2009; Ferrera et al., 1994), and that input is not stationary over development, so the system first learns from a low-passed world. The two-stream hypothesis (Mishkin et al., 1983; Goodale & Milner, 1992) and the emergence of ventral- and dorsal-like streams in a two-pathway network trained on one objective (Bakhtiari et al., 2021) motivate an architecture whose streams are defined by their subcortical-like inputs rather than by their cortical targets.

This paper asks two questions, in the order the originating study posed them (Manocchio, 2026): can two streams fed magnocellular- and parvocellular-like inputs diverge while optimising a single objective and producing shape-biased behaviour, and does such a system explain how the ventral stream can be texture-like while behaviour is shape-biased? We build it — two ResNet-18 trunks receiving the luminance and L−M channels of a cone-opponent image, with a developmental diet on the luminance channel (Fig. 1) — and examine it with linear probes and cue-conflict behaviour on the streams (Fig. 2), voxel-wise encoding models of eight subjects' 7T fMRI responses (Fig. 3) and the behavioural readout that combines the streams (Fig. 4). The streams diverge by different routes; the texture-biased one carries extrastriate cortex; and the integrated behaviour is shape-biased because a behavioural rate is a property of a readout over components, not a report of any one of them. Because the account predicts that properties bundled with shape bias need not travel with it, we test that too: robustness, agreement with human decisions and representational dimensionality all come apart from the rate (Fig. 5).

Relative to the originating thesis, every condition is trained with three seeds and every claim states its replication unit; the representational-similarity analysis that had found no stream-to-brain mapping is replaced by variance partitioning of encoding models, which removes the global stream preference that had defeated it; and the stream weighting the thesis fixed by hand is swept, so that the dependence of behaviour on the readout is measured rather than assumed.

**Results 2.1.**

The network splits its input once, before any convolution (Fig. 1a): each image is converted to the DKL cone-opponent space, stream 1 receives the luminance channel and stream 2 the L−M channel, and each is a ResNet-18 trunk. Under the developmental visual diet the luminance channel passes through a Gaussian blur (σ = 4 px) and the scale-free contrast-sensitivity cut of Lu et al. (2026) at a fixed age of 48 months, separating the transform from the trajectory (Methods 4.1). The trunks are initialised bit-identically — a control in which both streams receive the same image leaves all 120 tensor pairs identical after 150 epochs — so every stream difference below is attributable to the inputs, and a linear classifier reads out w·y₁ + (1−w)·y₂ of the two streams' penultimate features, with w = 0.5 during training.

The diet costs accuracy (Fig. 1c): the no-diet conditions converge to 0.70 validation top-1, the diet arm reaches 0.556 at its best saved checkpoint against 0.701 and, uniquely, degrades after its peak, ending at 0.445 (range 0.347–0.527), so behavioural and out-of-distribution results use each seed's best saved checkpoint, stated wherever the policy matters (Methods 4.7). Two single-stream controls trained with the identical diet — a ResNet-18 (11.2M parameters) and a ResNet-34 parameter-matched to the two-stream model (21.3M against 22.4M) — reach 0.599 and 0.623, so the accuracy cost of the split is not a capacity artefact (Fig. 4c).

**Results 2.2.**

Read out separately with a linear probe on the cue-conflict images, the two streams of the diet-trained network end on opposite sides of the shape–texture boundary: scored on exemplars the probe was not trained on, shape − texture accuracy is +0.337 for the magno stream and −0.168 for the parvo stream, means of three seeds, every seed on its side (Fig. 2a; +0.819 and −0.518 with exemplars shared; Methods 4.3); the magno stream decodes a new exemplar's texture at chance (0.08, against 0.0625). Behaviour agrees: read out alone at the final checkpoint, the policy of the cortical fits, the magno stream's cue-conflict shape bias is 0.878 and the parvo stream's 0.418, against 0.675 for the integrated readout (Table 1; best-checkpoint values in Supplementary Table S9) — the divergence the thesis reported on a single model per condition, now replicated across seeds.

The decomposition arises asymmetrically (Fig. 2a, Table 1). Scored on the untrained network with the same front end, most of the magno stream's shape bias is present before any weight is updated — the low-pass and contrast-sensitivity transform does it — at +0.210 of the trained +0.337 on held-out exemplars; the parvo stream begins nearly unbiased (−0.033) and acquires its texture preference over training. The shape-biased component starts in the input, the texture-biased component is built by learning, and what ordinary training does to the input's contribution is the decisive contrast: with the transform removed, the untrained luminance stream starts lower (+0.090) and training drives it across the sign flip to −0.122 — it does not merely fail to become shape-biased, it becomes texture-leaning — while the parvo stream ends texture-leaning whether or not the other stream is dieted (−0.168 against −0.118). Nor does the dieted input keep its bias alone: a single ResNet-18 trained on the magno stream's exact input and recipe ends at a rate of 0.557 and a penultimate-layer probe of −0.06 to −0.10 on held-out exemplars, where the same input as one of two streams ends at 0.878 and +0.26 to +0.27 — the shape-biased component survives training beside a second stream.

Which property of the input supplies the shape bias? Crossing the carrier a stream receives (achromatic luminance or chromatic L−M) with whether the developmental transform is applied to it gives four cells, two of which the swap and `parvo_highsf` conditions provide (Fig. 2b, c): on the probe, scored on held-out exemplars, the chromatic carrier gives −0.118 without the transform and −0.002 with it, the achromatic carrier −0.139 without and +0.337 with, so only the conjunction crosses zero, with an interaction of +0.36 over the additive prediction of the other three cells (−0.02) and no seed overlap between the conjunction cell (+0.333 to +0.340) and any other (at most +0.008); with exemplars shared the interaction is +0.73, and cue-conflict shape bias on the same four cells replicates it (Supplementary Table S3). The shape bias requires the developmental transform applied specifically to an achromatic channel — a relational fact about what a stream receives, not a property of a stream called “magno”.

Two qualifications belong here. First, cue-conflict shape bias is computed only over images a readout decides as the shape or the texture category, a count that varies four-fold across cells (165 to 598 of 1,200; Fig. 2d), so the three non-conjunction behavioural cells are read as “no strong preference”; the probe, which classifies every image, gives the same pattern, and so does a forced two-way estimator over every image, which compresses values toward 0.5 (the four cells read 0.44, 0.51, 0.35 and 0.76) while preserving every ordering this paper draws a conclusion from, the two no-preference cells trading places (Supplementary Table S9). Second, the diet-trained models are less accurate: we pre-registered the fatal outcome — a no-diet model at the diet's accuracy with a shape-biased magno stream — and retrained the no-diet network with per-epoch checkpointing, selecting by rule, in each of three seeds, the epoch nearest the diet's accuracy (0.546–0.554 against 0.556); matching accuracy closes 4.3% of the probe gap on held-out exemplars (1.5–5.9% across seeds; 7.2% with exemplars shared) and 6.1% of the behavioural gap (2.2–9.1%), and the matched magno stream stays on the texture side in every seed (−0.10 to −0.12; Fig. 2e, f).

**Results 2.3.**

The thesis had asked whether the streams align with different cortical streams by representational similarity analysis (Kriegeskorte et al., 2008) against seven regions of the Natural Scenes Dataset, and found that whichever stream was the more predictive correlated better with every region — a general factor that a correlation cannot separate from regional structure. We replaced the instrument with voxel-wise banded-ridge encoding models for each of the eight NSD subjects, one band per stream, partitioning every voxel's cross-validated explained variance into the part only the magno band accounts for (U_m) and the part only the parvo band does (U_p) (Methods 4.5); the per-voxel index (U_m − U_p)/(U_m + U_p), median over reliable voxels per ROI, across V1v → hV4 is Fig. 3a.

The two trained models sit on opposite sides of the global stream-preference axis — without the diet every retinotopic area is magno-explained, with it every area is parvo-explained — and that global difference, the general factor that defeated the correlational analysis, is not the result. The result is the shape of the profile: only the diet arm descends steeply from V1 to hV4, early visual cortex relatively more magno-explained and hV4 relatively more parvo-explained, the direction a luminance/low-spatial-frequency versus chromatic split predicts, and the descent belongs to the model whose global preference points the other way, which a model simply preferring one stream everywhere could not produce.

Quantified as the contrast hV4 − mean(V1v, V1d) of the per-ROI median (Fig. 3b; Extended Data Table 1), the gradient is −0.745 to −0.834 across the three diet-trained seeds against −0.049 to −0.096 without the diet, with the same separation on the raw statistic U_m − U_p, and all six comparisons reach the exact Wilcoxon floor for eight subjects (p = .0078) with complete seed separation. The per-subject Spearman ρ between hierarchical rank and stream preference is −0.964 to −0.973 with the diet and −0.46 to −0.66 without (Fig. 3c; Supplementary Table S1); the rank vector [1, 1, 2, 2, 3, 3, 4] admits exactly 630 labellings of the seven ROIs, and against that enumerable null — which calibrates the extremity of a result that was not pre-specified rather than testing a prediction — the diet arm's ρ is the single most extreme in every seed and on both statistics (p = 1/630 = .0016) while the no-diet arm is not (p = .019 to .092), so we claim nothing there.

The diet does not create the gradient; it decides what training does to the one the input supplies. An untrained network on the diet front end already sits at −0.544 (8/8 subjects, p = .0078), and on the no-diet front end at almost the same value (−0.479), so the two front ends alone impose no different gradient; training with the diet steepens it and training without it erodes it to near zero (Fig. 3b). Correcting each arm for the signed bias of the estimator itself — a simulated floor of the wrong sign, +0.04 with the diet and +0.05 without — leaves a paired displacement of −0.871 with the diet against −0.130 without, each non-zero in every subject, and an independently trained checkpoint from the thesis's own pipeline, with its progressive developmental schedule, gives −0.793.

Pushed through models fitted on natural scenes only, the cue-conflict images yield a per-voxel predicted shape-bias index whose magno − parvo difference is +1.53 to +1.57 with the diet against +0.08 to +0.11 without (8/8 subjects, p = .0078 in every seed; Fig. 3d; Supplementary Table S2) — a test that needs neither probe nor readout.

**The gradient survives its nuisance axes and the instrument's own bias.** Receptive-field size, eccentricity coverage and the noise ceiling all change from V1 to hV4, and the diet's magno stream is a low-pass instrument by construction; yet the diet gradient is negative within every one of NSD's five eccentricity bands and every pRF-size tertile while the no-diet arm stays near zero in each, it holds on continuous pRF eccentricity and size within subject, and the estimator's own simulated bias is small, flat across ROIs and of the wrong sign (Extended Data Fig. 3; Supplementary Tables S7 and S8; Supplementary Note S3).

**The gradient does not stop at hV4, and it does not turn.** Relabelling the stored fits with NSD's full atlases (Extended Data Fig. 4; Supplementary Table S10), the diet index runs from −0.05 in V1v through −0.81 in hV4 and then sits between −0.81 and −0.94 in every extrastriate region — ventral, lateral and dorsal alike, in the Kastner2015 atlas, NSD's stream parcels and the fLoc category regions — with every region-minus-V1 contrast negative in 8/8 subjects (p = .0078) and no region-minus-region contrast departing from zero under either arm (all p > .05). Beyond V3 the diet's magno band contributes about 0.01 unique variance while the parvo band's rises from 0.10 in V1v to 0.26 in PHC2 (Extended Data Fig. 4b): the texture-biased stream carries extrastriate cortex as a whole, and the thesis's magno→dorsal, parvo→ventral mapping fails for want of any magno variance to map.

Three things this section does not settle. The diet predicts BOLD worse, not better (median r² normalised by the noise ceiling 0.405 against 0.464 without the diet, in 8/8 subjects; Fig. 3e), so its case rests on stream differentiation and the gradient, not on prediction accuracy. The two front-end controls are degenerate on this instrument (swap: U_p = 0.006; `parvo_highsf`: U_m = 0.007; degenerate in every one of their three seeds, Methods 4.5), so only the diet on luminance yields two streams that both carry substantial unique cortical variance and a gradient between them (Fig. 3a). And the gradient was not pre-specified: it is the survivor of seven regional hypotheses, six of which failed — among them the thesis's magno→dorsal mapping, an hMT+ specificity and a foveal/peripheral contrast whose sign flipped when the blur was removed — reported because it alone survives removal of that manipulation, and read as sign and ordering rather than as a calibrated effect (Supplementary Notes S1 and S3; Extended Data Table 1 for the subject-01 influence check).

**Results 2.4.**

The two streams are oppositely biased and the texture-biased one carries extrastriate cortex; what remains is what the behavioural readout does with them. The integrated readout's cue-conflict shape bias is 0.675 ± 0.025 with the diet against 0.416 ± 0.020 without (mean ± s.d. over three seeds, final checkpoints, as in Table 1; the thesis's checkpoint reproduces at 0.697 against its reported 0.677). On the identical images and statistic the ten human observers give 0.958 ± 0.019 (range 0.919–0.987) and the Geirhos et al. (2018a) CNNs 0.429, 0.312 and 0.172, values our pipeline reproduces. The diet-trained magno stream, at 0.878, closes most of the distance from the CNN regime toward the observers without matching the lowest of them, and integration costs 0.203 of shape bias at this checkpoint policy (0.878 → 0.675; 0.248 at the best saved checkpoints; Fig. 4c, Supplementary Tables S4 and S9).

The integrated readout lands between its two components — an outcome two oppositely biased components nearly force, so not a prediction — and what can be measured is how much of the rate the readout controls. Sweeping w while holding y₁ and y₂ exactly fixed (Fig. 4a) runs cue-conflict shape bias from 0.40 to 0.90 over 21 values of w, three seeds and both checkpoint policies; w = 0.5 reproduces each trained model exactly, and the two streams alone sit at 0.41 and 0.78 on the forced two-way estimator, so the span is not an artefact of the decided-image denominator (Supplementary Table S9). Because the affine sweep is exact interpolation between two measured endpoints, under a rule fixed before the test we refitted a two-layer nonlinear head, and a linear head as a control for the refit itself, on the frozen features at each w (Fig. 4b): the nonlinear head's median span over the three best-checkpoint seeds is 0.466 against 0.508 for the frozen affine head (92% of the pre-registered baseline, 86% in the weakest seed, threshold 80%), the refitted-linear and nonlinear medians (0.461 and 0.466) agree within 0.01, and restricted to readouts within 5 points of peak validation accuracy the nonlinear span is 0.283 (0.26–0.31 across seeds): the span is a property of two oppositely biased components under any readout, and the extreme rates near w = 1 are bought with accuracy.

On the ladder of Fig. 4c the two streams alone bracket the integrated readout (0.903 and 0.400 around 0.655) and the single-stream controls trained with the identical diet lie between (0.567 and 0.625), inside the 0.058 that capacity alone moves the rate, so the ordering among these three models is attributed to capacity and no shape-bias advantage is claimed for the split.

Nothing here fits w to humans, and it could not be: fitted to each of the ten observers individually on a 0.001 grid, no observer's rate is reachable in any run (0 of 90 observer-runs, the closest approach missing the lowest observer by 0.027), and a weight fitted to an observer's trial-by-trial choices does not cross-validate (held-out agreement +0.026 against +0.023 for an arbitrary weight, better in 45 of 90). The rate is a free parameter of the readout, and nothing in this family of readouts selects the human operating point.

**The readout weight cortex prefers descends the hierarchy.** The readout's input z(w) = w·y₁ + (1 − w)·y₂ is a feature vector like any other, so for each w it was the design of a ridge encoding model of every voxel in NSD's general visual mask, and the w that maximises an ROI's median r² is the weight that region prefers (Methods 4.5; Extended Data Fig. 5; Extended Data Table 2). Under the diet that weight descends the hierarchy — 0.58 in V1 to 0.33–0.35 in every extrastriate group, ventral, lateral and dorsal alike, below V1 in 8/8 subjects (Δ = −0.252 [−0.283, −0.219], exact p = .0078) — and at the weight extrastriate cortex prefers the readout's rate is 0.56, below the trained 0.675 and far from the observers' 0.958. The weight that best reproduces the observers' trial-by-trial choices is not identified by these data: its median over observers is 0.67 (range 0.49–0.89), but each observer-run's bootstrap interval averages 0.69 wide on [0, 1] and covers the extrastriate optimum in 23 of 30 fits, only 2 of 30 fits beat a label-permutation null, and the fitted weight does not cross-validate (above), so we do not claim that cortex and behaviour prefer different weights (Extended Data Table 2). Without the diet the cortical gradient reverses (0.65 in V1 to 0.75–1.00 beyond hV4; Δ = +0.188 [+0.077, +0.285], 7/8). The curves are shallow (every ROI within 3% of its best r² at w = 0.5 with the diet and within 6% without) and their heights sit at the ridge's own reproducibility — the 1,024-dimensional concatenation, which every mixture is a linear map of, is itself exceeded by the best mixture in 36 of 112 subject × ROI × arm cells by a median 0.0019 r², more than the 0.0016 the sweep buys — so this is an ordering of optima and never an r² difference: the dissociation of Fig. 3 in the readout's own currency.

**Results 2.6.**

If shape bias is a property of a readout over components rather than of a representation, quantities the field treats as travelling with it need not. Over identical inputs, three do not (Fig. 5).

**Robustness.** The standard reading of Geirhos et al. (2018a, 2021) is that shape bias buys out-of-distribution robustness. We pre-registered that prediction and tested it on corruptions applied before the front end, scored in the models' own label space at each seed's best saved checkpoint (Fig. 5a; Extended Data Fig. 1; Supplementary Table S5). The diet-trained model is less robust on four of the five corruption families it was not trained on — uniform noise, high-pass, false colour and rotation (relative-accuracy AUC 0.634 against 0.729, 0.678 against 0.827, 0.854 against 0.938 and 0.574 against 0.615), with complete seed separation on each — equivalent within ±0.033 on phase scrambling, more robust only to low-pass (0.444 against 0.356), declared trained-in before the run because the diet is a low-pass blur, and not to contrast reduction (0.481 against 0.549) although its front end includes a contrast-sensitivity cut. Robustness appears exactly where it was trained in and nowhere else, the strongest available evidence that the nulls are real rather than a broken measurement. This does not contradict Geirhos et al., whose stylisation route yields shape bias together with robustness — it shows the coupling belongs to that route — and it differs from the source of the diet, whose title reports robust, shape-based vision: we reproduce the shape-based half (their released model 0.822, our diet-trained magno stream 0.878) and not the robust half, and Methods 4.6 lists three implementation differences any of which could carry the divergence.

**Agreement with human decisions.** Two decision-makers with the same marginal rate can agree on no individual image beyond what those rates force; error consistency — Cohen's κ on trial-by-trial agreement — subtracts that forced agreement (Geirhos et al., 2020), and across the ten observers the human–human ceiling is κ = 0.234. We pre-registered that the diet-trained model would be more human-consistent than its control; it is not, on either readout, and the sharpest true statement is stronger: our most shape-biased model, the diet-trained magno stream at 0.878, has, averaged over seeds, the lowest error consistency of the seven models compared (κ = 0.0082, 3.5% of the ceiling, seeds spanning −6.1% to 15.5%, against 7.3% for VGG-16 at shape bias 0.172; Supplementary Table S6); across all 189 readouts of the w sweep, with w chosen post hoc per run to maximise agreement, the best κ never exceeds 20.7% of the ceiling (Fig. 5b), and equivalence intervals fixed in advance exclude a κ change larger than 0.038 across the 0.71 span of shape bias the readouts cover. Cohen's κ cannot exceed a value fixed by the two marginals, so any rise of agreement with the rate is bounded by the measure itself (Geirhos et al., 2020). The measure does detect a correspondence when one is there — the same machinery recovers 24.1% of the split-half ceiling on whether an image resolves to either cue at all, against 8.7% on which cue is picked (Fig. 5c) — a positive control that invites a diet reading we do not make, because the crossed front-end 2×2 shows a train/test-matching pattern rather than a weights effect (Fig. 5d).

**Representational dimensionality.** The participation ratio of each stream's features on 1,000 NSD images per subject gives a magno/parvo ratio whose ordering across the four front ends is reproduced by a learning-free Gabor bank, so it tracks the bandwidth of the carrier a stream is handed; what the diet decides is which side of parity training ends on — every diet-trained seed ends magno-dominant (24/24 subject-seeds in each of three diet arms), including the `parvo_highsf` front end, which starts inverted in 8/8 subjects and which we had pre-registered would stay inverted, while every no-diet seed collapses to parity (Extended Data Fig. 2). No level is claimed, because the within-arm seed range exceeds the gaps between arms; and `parvo_highsf`'s second stream becomes the more dimensional stream while remaining texture-leaning (probe −0.139 on held-out exemplars, −0.271 shared), which separates dimensionality from shape bias.

**Discussion.**

**The two questions.** The thesis asked whether magnocellular- and parvocellular-driven streams inside one network could diverge while the network behaves shape-biased, and whether such a system bears on why the ventral stream can be texture-like while behaviour is not. To the first the answer is yes, replicated across seeds and with the mechanism located: divergence requires the developmental transform on an achromatic carrier, the shape-biased component starts in the input and is kept, and extended, by training only under the diet and beside a second stream, and the texture-biased component is built by learning. To the second the answer is an existence proof the thesis could not reach with its instruments: fitted to human cortex, the texture-biased stream's unique variance carries the extrastriate end of the hierarchy — ventral, lateral and dorsal alike — while behaviour, reading out from both, is shape-biased. Read as competing claims about a single representation the two cannot both be right; read as claims about a readout over components, they are not in competition.

**What the readout account licenses.** A model matching the human shape-bias rate is routinely described as having human-like representations; that inference fails twice over here. The same network holds components at +0.34 and −0.17 on held-out exemplars (+0.82 and −0.52 with exemplars shared) and behaves at 0.675, and one scalar over frozen representations moves the rate across most of the range the field's models occupy, so the rate does not identify a representation; and across readouts spanning a five-fold range of shape bias, trial-by-trial agreement with ten observers never exceeds 21% of the human–human ceiling in our networks and is lowest, 3.5%, for our most shape-biased stream, so the rate is at best a weak proxy for the decision process — as is agreement, read as the field reads it. Prior work established that a representation can hold more shape than its behaviour reveals (Hermann et al., 2020; Islam et al., 2021), that the measured rate depends on the test design (Tartaglini et al., 2022), that a prompt — a readout-level manipulation — steers it in vision–language models (Gavrikov et al., 2024), and that it tracks a network's spatial-frequency channel (Subramanian et al., 2023), the quantity the diet constrains; new here is the form of the dissociation — both representations held fixed, the rate moved by the readout alone, a refitted nonlinear head preserving the span — and its cortical grounding: the same mixing weight, fitted to cortex, prefers the magno-heavy end in V1 and the parvo-heavy end in every region beyond it (Extended Data Fig. 5); where human choices place it is not identified by these data.

**What a developmental constraint does (the BA network's fixed diet).** The intuitive account of low-pass developmental input is informational — coarse input shields the learner from texture, so it learns shape — and for the BA network's fixed transform our results do not support it: the transform removes bandwidth rather than adding structure, most of the shape bias it produces is present before training, and what the diet decides is whether learning builds on that bias or destroys it (on held-out exemplars, training drives the luminance stream from +0.09 to −0.12 without it and from +0.21 to +0.34 with it, and a second instrument replicates the side of parity rather than a level). This is a constraint on the solution learning converges to, not a better input representation, consistent with the thesis's observation that the transformed magno stream becomes less predictive of the category as the parvo stream becomes more so. The model's trajectory supports a version of the informational account in which the input's history matters and the information at test does not (Discussion).

**What comes apart, and what the diet costs.** Shape bias, robustness, dimensionality and human-likeness are discussed as a package; over identical inputs this system separates them three times, and a readout account says why it should: none of the three is a property of a readout. The diet is also expensive — less accurate (0.556 against 0.701), less stable late in training, less robust on four of five untrained-on corruptions, a worse predictor of BOLD — so nothing here recommends it as a training method; its interest is as a model of development, and the thesis's central mechanistic reading, that divergence and shape bias are bought by degrading the magno stream's evidence, survives in every instrument.

**Limitations (sentences condensed).** Beyond hV4 the index saturates near its bound in every atlas, so the extension establishes that the effect is hierarchy-wide but cannot order extrastriate regions against one another. The `parvo_highsf` condition varies architecture alongside the front end. The diet is a fixed 48-month front end rather than a trajectory, though a schedule-trained checkpoint behaves like it on every instrument we tried. The single-trunk subspaces are linear discriminants of one cue-conflict set, and their spans are lower bounds on what a readout can reach, not measurements of the best one.

## Supplementary Note S6 — the model's passages condensed in the main text

The main text was cut to the journal's length on 2026-10-24. Each paragraph it condensed is given here in full, as it stood before the cut, under the section it came from; the condensed paragraph keeps the claim, and every number it gives is taken from the passage here (`tmp_analysis/verify_v3_claims.py` checks both). *From 1. Introduction.*

If behaviour is shape-biased and the ventral stream texture-biased, either the two measures disagree about what shape bias is, or behaviour is not a readout of one representation. The second horn is a hypothesis about architecture that single-trunk models cannot express, and two facts make it concrete: the visual system receives a luminance-dominated, coarse magnocellular signal and a chromatically opponent, finer parvocellular one, separated before cortex and later recombined (Livingstone & Hubel, 1987; Kaplan & Shapley, 1982; Derrington et al., 1984; Nassi & Callaway, 2009; Ferrera et al., 1994), and that input is not stationary over development, so the system first learns from a low-passed world. The two-stream hypothesis (Mishkin et al., 1983; Goodale & Milner, 1992) and the emergence of ventral- and dorsal-like streams in a two-pathway network trained on one objective (Bakhtiari et al., 2021) motivate an architecture whose streams are defined by their subcortical-like inputs rather than by their cortical targets.

This paper asks two questions, in the order the originating study posed them (Manocchio, 2026): can two streams fed magnocellular- and parvocellular-like inputs diverge while optimising a single objective and producing shape-biased behaviour, and does such a system explain how the ventral stream can be texture-like while behaviour is shape-biased? We build it — two ResNet-18 trunks fed the luminance and L−M channels of a cone-opponent image, with a developmental diet on the luminance channel whose acuity and contrast sensitivity mature from birth to adulthood over training (Fig. 1) — and examine its streams with probes and cue-conflict behaviour (Fig. 2), encoding models of eight subjects' 7T fMRI responses (Fig. 3) and the readout that combines them (Fig. 4). The streams diverge by different routes, and the shape-biased one only when its input is achromatic and matured during training: the same network shown the same adult input from the first step, or given the ramp on its L−M channel instead, learns two texture-leaning streams. Only that conjunction divides the cortical hierarchy — the shape-biased stream carrying V1, the stronger, texture-leaning stream extrastriate cortex — and tracks which images human observers resolve. The integrated behaviour is shape-biased because a behavioural rate is a property of a readout over components, not a report of any one of them. Robustness, error consistency with human observers and representational dimensionality come apart from the rate, as the account predicts (Fig. 5).

*From 2.1 The model, and what the developmental trajectory costs.*

The network splits its input once, before any convolution (Fig. 1a): each image is converted to the DKL cone-opponent space, stream 1 receives the luminance channel and stream 2 the L−M channel, and each is a ResNet-18 trunk. Under the developmental diet the luminance channel passes through the acuity blur and the contrast-sensitivity cut of the developmental visual diet of Lu et al. (2026), both evaluated at one age that rises linearly with the training step from birth to 300 months (Methods 4.1). Every evaluation uses the endpoint, at which the acuity blur is inert and the contrast cut is an adult's, so at test the magno stream sees nearly clean luminance. The trunks are initialised bit-identically, so every stream difference below is attributable to the inputs, and a linear classifier reads out w·y₁ + (1−w)·y₂ of the two streams' penultimate features, with w = 0.5 during training, under one cross-entropy on that output and no loss on either stream.

The decisive control holds the diet at its 300-month endpoint from the first step: the same architecture, recipe, seeds and test input, without the developmental trajectory. The originating study's network (“BA network”) held a fixed mixture of ages instead — a σ = 4 px blur, about a 3-month-old's acuity, and a 48-month-old's contrast sensitivity — and is reported beside the model throughout, with its no-diet arm; trained with our recipe, the same fixed diet separates the recipe from the trajectory. Networks were trained on MiniEcoset (112 classes; Thorat & Kietzmann, 2024) for 50 epochs with AdamW, a cosine schedule, an exponential moving average of the weights and label smoothing, three seeds per condition (Methods 4.2); the BA network keeps the thesis's 150-epoch recipe.

The developmental trajectory costs accuracy, not stability (Fig. 1c): the model reaches 0.683 ± 0.002 validation top-1 at its final checkpoint, within 0.9 points of its best epoch in every seed, against 0.764 ± 0.003 for the control on the identical test input. The fixed BA diet reaches 0.665 ± 0.003 under the same recipe and 0.556 at its best saved checkpoint under the thesis's, after which it degrades (final 0.445). The trajectory therefore adds 1.8 points and 0.067 of shape bias (training-time evaluation) over the fixed diet under one recipe, and gives up 8.1 points against the network that learned the same input without the trajectory. A trajectory stopping at 48 months (one seed) is no better than the fixed diet (0.664 top-1, shape bias 0.637): the input has to reach adult acuity, not only start coarse. Every result for the model and its control below is from final checkpoints (Methods 4.7).

*From 2.2 The two streams specialise in opposite directions, by different routes.*

Read out separately with a linear probe on the cue-conflict images, the two streams end on opposite sides of the shape–texture boundary: on exemplars the probe was not trained on, shape − texture accuracy is +0.316 for the magno stream and −0.156 for the parvo stream, means of three seeds, every seed on its side and every seed's 5–95% interval over draws excluding zero (Fig. 2a; +0.578 and −0.454 with exemplars shared; Methods 4.3). This is the BA network's split (+0.337 and −0.168), at 0.683 top-1 against that network's 0.445 at the same, final, checkpoints. Behaviour agrees: read out alone, the magno stream's cue-conflict shape bias is 0.850 and the parvo stream's 0.477, against 0.682 for the integrated readout (Table 1).

Both components are built by learning, and the input's trajectory decides the sign of one (Fig. 2a, Table 1). Untrained, the magno stream on the model's test input sits where undieted luminance does (+0.087 against +0.090 on held-out exemplars), and the parvo stream begins nearly unbiased (−0.033). Training on the maturing input adds +0.229 to the magno stream — nearly twice what training added in the BA network (+0.127), whose fixed blur put +0.210 into the input before any learning — and −0.123 to the parvo stream. Training on the same adult input from the first step drives the magno stream the other way, across the sign flip to −0.177, below its own parvo stream (−0.069), and the integrated readout to 0.374, near the BA no-diet arm's 0.416. The two networks see identical images at test and differ only in whether the magno input matured during training: the magno stream's shape bias is a property not of the input it is tested on but of the input it learned from. The two magno streams sit on opposite sides of parity at every epoch evaluated during training (shape bias 0.63–0.92 against 0.32–0.495 over three seeds each; training-time evaluation), so the bias is present from the first evaluation, after one epoch at ages up to 6 months (top-1 0.09), and is kept as the input sharpens. Whether the coarse-to-fine direction matters, or only exposure to coarse input, these runs cannot say (Discussion, Limitations). It is not a filter on texture either: on held-out exemplars the model's magno stream decodes texture at 0.148 (chance 0.0625; 0.078 for the BA network's blurred stream) and shape at 0.464, so the stream holds texture information and leans to shape.

Which property of the input carries the effect? Crossing the carrier (achromatic luminance or chromatic L−M) with the developmental ramp gives four cells. They are achromatic with the ramp (+0.316), achromatic with the adult input from the start (−0.177), chromatic without the ramp (−0.156 in the model, −0.069 in the control) and chromatic with the ramp: the channel swap, which applies the same ramp to L−M with luminance untransformed (three seeds; −0.016). The ramp moves the achromatic stream by +0.49 and the chromatic stream by +0.05, which leaves it below parity; the carrier × ramp interaction is +0.44 on held-out exemplars (+0.73 with exemplars shared), and no seed of the conjunction cell (+0.30 to +0.33) overlaps a seed of any other cell (at most −0.011). Only the ramp on an achromatic carrier crosses zero, as only the BA network's fixed transform on an achromatic carrier did (interaction +0.36 on held-out exemplars; Supplementary Table S3). The swap reproduces its control on every other measure as well: 0.760 top-1, an integrated cue-conflict rate of 0.389 and both streams below parity (Table 1). Applied to the chromatic channel, the trajectory buys neither the shape bias nor the accuracy cost.

Two qualifications belong here. First, cue-conflict shape bias is computed only over images a readout decides, a count that varies across readouts (426 to 580 of 1,200 in the model, 209 to 673 in the control; seed means); the probe, which classifies every image, and a forced two-way estimator over every image preserve every ordering this paper draws a conclusion from (forced estimator: magno 0.730, integrated 0.648, parvo 0.520; control 0.393, 0.383, 0.471), but the parvo stream sits within 0.03 of parity on both estimators and on opposite sides of it, so its side is read from the probe. Second, the BA study pre-registered its fatal outcome as a no-diet network at the diet's accuracy with a shape-biased magno stream, and closed it with an accuracy-matched retraining (Supplementary Note S4). The same worry applies here, since the model is the less accurate network (0.683 against 0.764), and the control's own training answers it: at the epoch where its top-1 matches the model's final 0.683 (epoch 7 of 50 in each of the three seeds), its magno stream's shape bias is 0.41–0.43, and at no epoch of any seed does it reach parity (highest 0.495; training-time evaluation).

*From 2.3 In cortex, the magno stream carries V1 and the texture-biased stream the rest of the hierarchy.*

The thesis's representational similarity analysis (Kriegeskorte et al., 2008) against seven NSD regions found that whichever stream was the more predictive correlated better with every region, a general factor that a correlation cannot separate from regional structure. We replaced it with voxel-wise banded-ridge encoding models for each of the eight NSD subjects, one band per stream, partitioning each voxel's cross-validated explained variance into the part only the magno band accounts for (U_m) and the part only the parvo band does (U_p) (Methods 4.5); Fig. 3a plots the per-voxel index (U_m − U_p)/(U_m + U_p), median over reliable voxels per ROI, across V1v → hV4.

The profile crosses zero, and only with the maturing input. In the model the index runs from +0.55 in V1v, magno-explained, through zero at V2v to −0.76 in hV4, parvo-explained (Fig. 3a): the magno stream carries V1 and the parvo stream the ventral end of the retinotopic hierarchy (in V1, U_m 0.10–0.11 against U_p 0.03–0.04, U_m larger in 77–82% of voxels and in 8/8 subjects in every seed; in hV4, 0.015–0.020 against 0.13). The control, on the identical test input, is magno-explained everywhere and nearly flat (+0.81 in V1v, +0.73 in hV4), as are the channel swap (+0.85, +0.83) and the BA no-diet network (+0.91, +0.83); the BA network is parvo-explained from V1 on and descends without crossing (−0.00 to −0.84).

Quantified as the contrast hV4 − mean(V1v, V1d) of the per-ROI median (Fig. 3b; Extended Data Table 1), the gradient is −1.259 to −1.291 across the three seeds against −0.062 to −0.086 for the control's, each seed against the control seed of the same index at the exact Wilcoxon floor for eight subjects (p = .0078, 8/8), with the same separation on the raw statistic U_m − U_p (−0.186 to −0.193 against −0.003 to −0.007). It is steeper than the BA network's −0.745 to −0.833 in 8/8 subjects (mean Δ −0.48, p = .0078). The per-subject Spearman ρ between hierarchical rank and stream preference is −0.964 to −0.973 in every seed, against −0.29 to −0.41 for the control (Fig. 3c). The channel swap, the same ramp on the chromatic carrier, has no gradient either (−0.019 normalised, +0.002 raw, seed means; ρ = −0.22), and the model's lies below it in 8/8 subjects (p = .0078): in cortex as in behaviour, only the ramp on the achromatic carrier departs from the control.

The trajectory does not create the gradient; it decides what training does to the one the input supplies. An untrained network on the same test input already descends (−0.480 normalised, −0.195 raw, 8/8 subjects, p = .0078 on both; −0.544 and −0.479 for untrained networks on the BA network's two front ends). Training on the maturing input steepens it on the normalised index (to −1.27) and holds it on the raw difference (−0.19), so under the rule that a cortical claim needs both statistics (Methods 4.7) we claim only that training on the maturing input preserves it; training on the adult input from the first step erases it on both (−0.070; −0.004, seed means).

Pushed through models fitted on natural scenes only, the cue-conflict images give a predicted magno − parvo shape-bias difference of +1.32 to +1.34 in the model against +0.07 for the control and −0.01 for the channel swap (8/8 subjects, seed means; Fig. 3d), a test that needs neither probe nor readout. It is smaller than the BA network's +1.53 to +1.57 (8/8), in the direction of the two magno streams' own rates (0.850 against 0.878).

**The gradient continues past hV4 and does not turn.** On NSD's full atlases (Extended Data Fig. 4), relative to V1, hV4 sits at −1.12 and every extrastriate group lower — ventral (VO, PHC) −1.25, lateral (LO, TO) −1.27, dorsal (V3A/B, IPS0–2) −1.22 — each in 8/8 subjects (p = .0078). The ventral and lateral groups sit a further 0.14–0.15 below hV4 (p = .039 and .016, uncorrected), and no contrast among the three extrastriate groups departs from zero (all p ≥ .08). Beyond V3 the magno band contributes about 0.013 unique variance against 0.149 for the parvo band: the texture-biased stream carries extrastriate cortex as a whole, and the thesis's magno→dorsal mapping fails for want of any magno variance to map. Nor is the model's gradient an eccentricity or receptive-field-size contrast in disguise. Among voxels of matched eccentricity it is negative in each of NSD's five eccentricity bands (−0.58 in the foveal band to −1.33 beyond 4°), and among voxels of matched pRF size in each size tertile (−1.26 to −1.32), below the control's in every band and tertile (7/7 or 8/8 subjects; 6/6 in the smallest tertile), while the control and the channel swap stay near zero in each (Extended Data Fig. 3; Supplementary Note S3). Nor does the estimator manufacture it. Simulated with the model's fitted feature geometry, the measured noise ceilings and a true magno − parvo difference of zero everywhere, the recovered contrast is small and of the wrong sign (+0.018, p = .11; +0.066 on the control's geometry). Corrected by each arm's own floor, the model's gradient is −1.277 against −0.127 for the control, both non-zero in 8/8 subjects, and the model's is the steeper in every subject (p = .0078; Supplementary Note S3).

Three things this section does not settle. First, the trajectory buys the gradient at a small cost in BOLD prediction against its control (noise-ceiling-normalised median r² 0.471 against 0.494, 8/8 subjects), although the model predicts BOLD better than the BA network does (0.395, 8/8; Fig. 3e). Second, the gradient was not pre-specified: it is the survivor of seven regional hypotheses tested on the BA network, six of which failed, and is read as sign and ordering rather than as a calibrated effect (Supplementary Notes S1 and S3). Third, “texture-biased” names the stream, not the reason cortex prefers it. Both streams receive near-full-bandwidth input at test, which removes the BA network's confound between texture bias and the magno stream's blur; but in the model the stream carrying extrastriate cortex is both the texture-leaning one and the chromatic one, so the design cannot separate texture bias from the carrier. It is also, in every network, the trunk that carries the task (Results 2.2), so task load is confounded with texture bias too; what only the model has is a V1 carried by the other trunk.

*From 2.4 Behaviour reads out from both streams, and the rate is a property of the readout.*

A readout of two oppositely biased components is nearly forced to land between them; what can be measured is how much of the rate it controls. Sweeping w while holding y₁ and y₂ exactly fixed (Fig. 4a) runs cue-conflict shape bias from 0.48 to 0.85 over 21 values of w and three seeds, and w = 0.5 reproduces each trained model exactly. The control's curve is flat (0.37–0.42), and so is the channel swap's (0.38–0.40): with both streams texture-leaning there is nothing for the weight to move. Because the sweep interpolates between two measured endpoints, we refitted a two-layer head on the frozen features at each w under the rule the BA study fixed in advance (Fig. 4b): its median span is 0.395 against 0.371 for the frozen affine head (106%; weakest seed 96%; threshold 80%), and 0.241 when restricted to readouts within 5 points of peak validation accuracy. The span is a property of two oppositely biased components under any readout, and the extreme rates are bought with accuracy. Nothing here fits w to humans, and it could not: no observer's rate is reachable in any run (0 of 30 observer-runs), and a weight fitted to an observer's trial-by-trial choices barely cross-validates (held-out agreement 0.039 against 0.031 for an arbitrary weight, above it in 17 of 30 observer-runs).

**The readout weight cortex prefers descends the hierarchy.** For each w, the readout's input z(w) = w·y₁ + (1 − w)·y₂ was the design of a ridge encoding model of every voxel in NSD's general visual mask, and the w that maximises an ROI's median r² is the weight that region prefers (Methods 4.5; Extended Data Fig. 5; Extended Data Table 2). In the model that weight falls from 0.65 in V1 to 0.30–0.35 in every extrastriate group, below V1 in 8/8 subjects (Δ = −0.323 [−0.371, −0.269], exact p = .0078); at the extrastriate weight the network's own rate would be 0.60, at V1's 0.75. In the control the gradient reverses (0.43 in V1 to 0.62–0.78 beyond hV4; higher in 8/8 subjects, p = .0078), and so it does in the channel swap (0.38 in V1 to 0.62–1.00; higher in 7/8 subjects and tied in one, p = .016). The weight that best reproduces the observers' choices is not identified by these data (median 0.79 over observers, but per-fit bootstrap intervals average 0.70 wide on [0, 1] and only 10 of 30 fits beat a permutation null), so we do not claim that cortex and behaviour prefer different weights. The curves are shallow — every ROI is within 2% of its best r² at w = 0.5 — so this is an ordering of optima, not an r² difference (Supplementary Note S4).

This is the mechanism we propose for the paradox: a system holding a shape-biased and a texture-biased component, whose behaviour reads out from both, will look shape-biased behaviourally and texture-biased wherever the texture-biased component dominates — and in this model of the subcortical split, the component that dominates beyond V1 is the texture-biased one, and the shape-biased component exists at all only if its input matured during training. It is an existence proof with a cortically grounded demonstration, not a measurement of the human readout; Results 2.5 tests it against the observers' own decisions.

*From 2.6 Properties that do not follow from shape bias.*

**Robustness.** The standard reading of Geirhos et al. (2018a, 2021) is that shape bias buys out-of-distribution robustness. The BA study pre-registered that prediction for its diet and it failed (Supplementary Table S5); the model repeats the failure against a control that sees the same test images (Methods 4.6; Fig. 5a; Extended Data Fig. 1). In relative-accuracy area under the curve, the model is less robust than its control on four of the five corruption families neither was trained on — uniform noise (−0.135), high-pass (−0.136), false colour (−0.084) and rotation (−0.040), every seed of the model below every seed of the control (exact Mann–Whitney p = 0.10, the floor for three seeds against three) — within 0.01 of it on phase scrambling (+0.009), and more robust only to low-pass (+0.078), the family its trajectory trains in, although at test its own input is no longer blurred; contrast, the other trained-in family, is level (−0.004, seeds interleaved). Robustness appears where it was trained in and nowhere else. The coupling belongs to the stylisation route of Geirhos et al., not to shape bias; against the source of the diet we reproduce its shape-based half and not its robust half (Supplementary Note S4; Methods 4.6).

**Agreement with human decisions.** Error consistency — Cohen's κ on trial-by-trial agreement — subtracts the agreement two decision-makers' marginal rates force (Geirhos et al., 2020); across the ten observers the human–human ceiling is κ = 0.234. In the BA network the most shape-biased readout, its magno stream at 0.878, had the lowest error consistency of seven models compared (3.5% of the ceiling; Supplementary Table S6). The model reverses that observation: its magno stream (0.850) is its most human-consistent readout, at 17.3% of the ceiling, above its integrated readout (13.2%), its parvo stream (10.3%) and the control (11.9% integrated, 12.6% magno), and the best of its 63 sweep readouts reaches 29.0%. Neither observation is about shape bias. Across the seven models now compared, κ rises with the rate by 0.019 [0.011, 0.027] (90% interval) over the 0.68 span of shape bias they cover, 8.1% of the ceiling; Cohen's κ cannot exceed a value fixed by the two marginals, so part of that rise is the measure's own bound (Geirhos et al., 2020; Klein et al., 2025). At a matched rate it does not separate our seeds: the three magno streams sit at 0.871, 0.842 and 0.838 and reach 5.1%, 25.6% and 21.3% of the ceiling. The BA study's pre-registered prediction, that the diet would make the network more human-consistent than its control, is not supported: the model's integrated readout reaches 13.2% of the ceiling (seeds 9.4–17.7%) and the control's 11.9% (10.3–13.2%), with the seeds interleaved (exact Mann–Whitney p = 1.0; magno streams 17.3% against 12.6%, p = 0.70). The measure does detect a correspondence when one is there, and here the trajectory supplies it. Asked not which cue the observers pick on an image but whether it resolves to either cue at all, the model's readout recovers 22.3% of the split-half ceiling on held-out images (seeds 19.6–24.3%), against 9.0% for the control (6.6–10.4%) and 8.2% for the channel swap (7.1–10.4%), every model seed above every seed of both (exact Mann–Whitney p = 0.10, the floor); on which cue is picked the three are interleaved (13.3%, 10.4% and 9.5%; p ≥ 0.70; Fig. 5c). The readout weight that recovers it is magno-heavy (0.91–0.98, chosen on held-out images). The BA network showed the same contrast against its no-diet arm (24.1% against 9.2%), but crossing weights and test input removed it in both directions (−0.6% and −3.7%; Fig. 5d): it measured whether a network is tested on the input it was trained on, not the diet. The model and its control are each tested on the input they finished training on, and on the same one, so the difference between them belongs to the trajectory.

**Representational dimensionality.** The magno/parvo ratio of the streams' participation ratios on NSD images ends above parity in every subject and seed of the model (24/24; seed means 1.88–1.98) and below it in 23 of its control's 24 (seed means 0.89–0.95), from the same untrained starting point (1.63) and the same test input (Extended Data Fig. 2). What the trajectory decides is which side of parity training ends on, as the BA diet did against its no-diet network (seed means 1.60–2.51 against 0.91–0.99). That dimensionality is not shape bias is shown twice. The channel swap also ends above parity (24/24; seed means 1.27–2.15) with neither stream shape-biased, and for the opposite reason: its ramped chromatic stream contracts (participation ratio 52–86 against 116–124 in the control, seed means) while its luminance stream stays where the control's is (109–113 against 108–110), whereas in the model the ramped luminance stream expands (272–281). And the BA network's `parvo_highsf` control's second stream becomes the more dimensional stream while remaining texture-leaning (probe −0.139 on held-out exemplars).

*From 2.5 Predicted cortex is not texture-biased on the cue-conflict images; in the same voxels, the two bands carry opposite evidence.*

Everything so far links cortex to the model on natural images and the model to the observers on the cue-conflict images; nothing yet links predicted cortex to the observers on the same images, and that is the link the paradox is about. We therefore pushed the 1,200 cue-conflict images through each subject's fitted encoding model (Methods 4.8) and read the predicted response pattern of every region against sixteen category templates — the predicted patterns of natural MiniEcoset photographs of the same sixteen categories, none of them cue-conflict images. For each image, the predicted pattern's correlation with its shape category's template minus that with its texture category's template is the region's shape margin, and the fraction of images on which that margin is positive is the region's shape-bias rate, the cortical analogue of the behavioural rate (0.68 for the model's readout, 0.96 for the observers).

The prediction fails at its first step (Fig. 6a). The predicted extrastriate representations are not texture-biased by this criterion: the cortical shape-bias rate of the model's joint fit is 0.53 in hV4 (8/8 subjects above 0.5), 0.47 in ventral (8/8 below, every seed) and 0.50 in lateral cortex (5/8 below), against 0.57–0.60 in V1–V3; every rate lies below the network's behavioural rate of 0.68, but nowhere near the texture dominance the account predicted (P-C1 not met). The control (0.40–0.51 across regions) and the channel swap (0.39–0.51) sit lower than the model in every region, the untrained network at 0.49–0.55 and the input floor at 0.48–0.52: on these stylised images the nearest-template criterion places every predicted representation near chance, and the trajectory moves it towards shape, not away from it. By the plan's decision table the reading of record is `CORTEX_NOT_TEXTURE_BIASED_ON_THESE_IMAGES`: the paradox is not reproduced in predicted cortex, and the reading is the same in the three registered sensitivity analyses (Spearman evidence, V1 from the streams atlas, all voxels).

The evidence is there, but not beyond the input floor (Fig. 6b). The predicted shape margin tracks which cue the observers chose in every region: 8/8 subjects positive in hV4, ventral and lateral cortex (exact Wilcoxon p = .0039), at 15.8% of the residual ceiling in hV4, 9.4% in ventral and 10.3% in lateral cortex, and above the within-cell permutation null in V1–hV4 (p ≤ .0015) though not at the registered p < .01 in ventral (p = .029) or lateral cortex (p = .013). The input floor — the same encoders on oriented-energy features of the two streams' inputs — carries 11.5% in hV4 and 13.0–13.8% beyond it: the model exceeds it in 7/8 subjects in hV4 (p = .0078) and in 0/8 and 1/8 in ventral and lateral cortex. Whatever the predicted extrastriate pattern knows about the observers' choices on these images, oriented energy knows as well (P-C2 not met). The cue margin and the per-image resolution rate (P-C2b) correspond at 8.7–9.5% of ceiling, 8/8 subjects positive, but do not beat the permutation null (p = .12–.18).

What does hold is the decomposition (Fig. 6c). In the same voxels of the same fit, the luminance band's contribution to the predicted pattern is shape-biased in every region — rates 0.66 in V1 rising to 0.70–0.73 beyond V3 — and the L−M band's is texture-biased, 0.42–0.51, with the joint fit between them; this holds in 8/8 subjects, 24/24 seed-cells and every one of the seven regions (P-C3 met). The predicted cortical image of a cue-conflict stimulus is, as the model's own layers are (Results 2.4), a sum of a shape-biased and a texture-biased component; what the test does not find is the texture-biased sum the account predicted beyond hV4.

The trajectory does not add to the correspondence (Fig. 6d). On the resolution target, every model seed (7.0–9.3% of ceiling, extrastriate mean) lies below every control seed (9.5–13.1%) and every swap seed (10.0–12.0%; exact Mann–Whitney, 3 v 3, U = 0: complete separation in the direction opposite to the prediction, one-sided p = .05), and on the shape target the arms interleave (model 8.4–15.2%, control 7.8–11.1%, swap 8.9–12.4%; p = 1.0): the behavioural result of Fig. 5c, where only the model tracked which images resolve, is not recovered from predicted cortex (P-C4 not met). The regional profile (P-C5) is a rise from V1 (13.9%) to hV4 (15.8%) and a fall beyond (9.4–10.8%), against a median encoder r² falling from 0.19 to 0.09; the floor's profile is the reverse (9.7–11.5% in V1–hV4, 13.0–13.8% beyond), so beyond hV4 the predicted patterns and the stylised inputs' oriented energy agree with the observers through the same images. Fig. 6e shows hV4 image by image: the subject-mean residualised margin against the observers' residualised shape rate, r = 0.108 [0.050, 0.155] under the exemplar-cluster bootstrap, 17% of the ceiling.

*From 3. Discussion.*

**Where the account fails.** The registered cortex-to-decision test (Results 2.5) asked predicted cortex to show the paradox itself: extrastriate representations texture-biased by the cue-conflict criterion that still track, image by image, the cue the observers chose. It does not. The predicted representations are not texture-biased on these images (rates 0.47–0.53 beyond V3, against 0.39–0.51 for the control and the swap), their correspondence with the observers beyond hV4 is matched by the oriented energy of the stylised inputs, and the trajectory adds nothing to it; only the decomposition survives — in the same voxels, the luminance band's contribution is shape-biased and the L−M band's texture-biased in every region, subject and seed. The readout account therefore keeps its behavioural half and its cortical correspondence on natural images, and not the claim that the texture-dominated cortical image of a cue-conflict stimulus carries the shape evidence; whether that is a fact about extrastriate cortex or about reading predicted responses to stylised images against natural templates, the test cannot say.

**What the readout account licenses.** A model matching the human shape-bias rate is routinely described as having human-like representations; here that inference fails twice. The same network holds components at +0.32 and −0.16 on held-out exemplars and behaves at 0.682, and one scalar over frozen representations moves the rate across most of the range the field's models occupy, so the rate does not identify a representation; and trial-by-trial agreement with the observers never exceeds 29% of the human–human ceiling in our networks and rises with the rate only as far as the bound on κ allows, so the rate is at best a weak proxy for the decision process. Prior work established that a representation can hold more shape than its behaviour reveals (Hermann et al., 2020; Islam et al., 2021), that the measured rate depends on the test design (Tartaglini et al., 2022) and on a prompt in vision–language models (Gavrikov et al., 2024), and that it tracks a network's spatial-frequency channel (Subramanian et al., 2023); new here is the form of the dissociation — both representations held fixed, the rate moved by the readout alone — and its cortical grounding: the same mixing weight, fitted to cortex, prefers the magno-heavy end in V1 and the parvo-heavy end beyond it (Extended Data Fig. 5). The test of the account where it has to hold — on the observers' own decisions, against the predicted cortical representations of the same images — is Results 2.5.

**What a developmental trajectory does, and what it costs.** The intuitive account of low-pass developmental input is informational: coarse input shields the learner from texture, so it learns shape. Our results support a version of it in which the input's history matters and the information at test does not. The model and its control see the same images at test, and the untrained network on that input carries no more shape bias than undieted luminance; what differs is whether the magno stream's early learning happened on coarse, low-contrast input. Its route runs through the competition between the trunks: the coarse early input hands the task to the chromatic trunk, and the luminance trunk, learning beside it from a world that sharpens, ends the weaker and shape-biased; the swap shows that being the weaker trunk is not enough (Results 2.2). The trajectory is thus a constraint on the solution learning converges to, not a filter on what the network sees — the BA network's fixed 48-month transform conflated the two, putting most of its magno stream's shape bias into the input (+0.210 of +0.337 before training) at a cost in accuracy (0.556), stability and BOLD prediction (0.395). The trajectory costs less: 8.1 points of top-1 against its control, robustness to corruptions it was not trained on, and 0.024 of noise-ceiling-normalised r². Nothing here recommends it as a training method for accuracy; its interest is as a model of development, and its prediction is developmental: a visual system whose early input is coarse should show a stream-level shape bias that a system given adult input from the start, on the same images, does not.

**Limitations.** The load-bearing cortical result rests on eight subjects from one dataset and is read as sign and ordering only. The mapping between our chromatic stream and texture-selective cortex is by explanatory role, not content: nothing here asserts that the ventral stream is chromatic, the prediction that the chromatic stream should win in colour-biased cortex fails in the BA network (pooled VO +0.005, bounded within ±0.017), and substituting the koniocellular axis makes it worse (Supplementary Note S2). In every network the trunk that carries extrastriate cortex is also the one that carries the task, so the cortical result cannot separate texture bias from task load; trunks matched in accuracy would. One trajectory shape was tested — linear in age from birth to 300 months over 50 epochs — together with one seed of a trajectory stopping at 48 months; how the result depends on the shape and length of the trajectory is open, and so is whether its direction matters: the control removes both the coarse-to-fine order and the exposure to coarse input, and a reversed or age-shuffled schedule tested at the same endpoint would separate them. No single-stream network was trained with the trajectory, so whether its shape bias, like the BA diet's, survives only beside a second stream is untested. The model's recipe (AdamW, cosine schedule, weight averaging, label smoothing, 50 epochs) differs from the thesis's, so comparisons with the BA network mix diet and recipe; the BA diet trained with the model's recipe (0.665 top-1, shape bias 0.620, gradient −0.735) separates the two. The model of record was changed after its first seed was seen; the rule for the change, and the control that could have blocked it, were fixed before seeds 1–2 and the control were trained (Methods 4.2). Whether a two-stream architecture specialises on its own is untestable here, because the trunks are bit-identical at initialisation. Human comparisons are against ten published observers and of aggregate rate; the readout weight is a hyperparameter that nothing selects. The cue-conflict set reuses its exemplars, so the probe is reported with exemplars held out. The plans committed before their analyses ran on the BA network, and every outcome, failures included, are summarised in Supplementary Note S5. Of the main study's four pre-registered predictions, two failed on the BA network and are reported as failures; on the model, the robustness prediction fails again and the error-consistency one is not supported (model and control seeds interleaved). The cortex-to-decision test (Results 2.5) reads predicted, not measured, responses to stylised images outside the encoders' training distribution, against templates from natural photographs; it was registered before its runs were submitted (Supplementary Note S5), its reading of record is a failure, and a defect in its permutation null on the shape target, found after the run, is disclosed and corrected in Methods 4.8. Claims deliberately not made are listed in Supplementary Note S1.
