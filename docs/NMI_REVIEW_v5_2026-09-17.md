## Consensus Weaknesses

1. **[Abstract; Discussion "What the readout account licenses"; Supplementary Note S1, first bullet]** The abstract's cortical clause no longer describes what the paper found. It reads "in our single trunks extrastriate cortex loads on the texture-leaning units". Three facts in the paper undercut it. (i) The property that distinguishes the authors' trunks is the contrast *relative to V1* (Results 2.4: "carries every extrastriate group relative to V1"). In absolute terms, the texture-leaning half carries every region, V1 included, in ConvNeXt-B (−0.09 to −0.45) and ViT-B/16 (−0.13 to −0.31), and V1 through ventral cortex in ResNet-50 (ED Table 15, lower block). (ii) The V1-relative contrast is absent in all three ImageNet networks fitted (+0.24, p = .039; +0.22, 2/8; −0.08, 6/8, p = .25). (iii) It is carried by unique variances of 0.006–0.04 (ViT-B/16 up to 0.09), with 64–96% of explained variance shared between the halves. Under the pre-specified subspace rule the cortical predictions failed. The Results and the Fig. 6 legend state all of this. The abstract states none of it, and Supplementary Note S1's first bullet still names only ResNet-50 as the failure.
2. **[Results 2.4 cortex paragraph; ED Tables 14–15; Fig. 6c]** **The unit-split index has no null, and the new fits leave training set and architecture confounded.** ED Table 14 shows that almost no units are shape-leaning in absolute terms: 0.00–0.02 of units in the six ImageNet networks, and 0.05–0.17 even in the authors' trunks. The "shape-leaning half" is therefore the half whose d_j = η²_shape − η²_texture lies nearest zero. That half plausibly includes the units least driven by either cue. A drive difference of this kind would make the texture-leaning half carry *every* region, which is what ConvNeXt-B and ViT-B/16 show, V1 included. The V1-relative contrast in the authors' trunks changes sign across regions (rgb_tsn V1 +0.17, extrastriate −0.14 to −0.15), so it is not explained by drive alone. The absolute loading that the abstract states is uncalibrated, though. Separately, the contrast is present in the two networks trained on MiniEcoset (ResNet-18, ResNet-34) and absent in the three trained on ImageNet (ResNet-50, ConvNeXt-B, ViT-B/16). Training set and scale are confounded, and Results 2.4's own scope ("in the networks trained on our images") points at the training set without testing it. The zoo already contains the ImageNet ResNet-18 and ResNet-34, the same architectures as the authors' trunks.
3. **[Results 2.3 "Three things this section does not settle"; Fig. 3a legend; Methods 4.5 last sentence; Supplementary Note S1; ED Table 4; Fig. 2b–c]** **The text did not follow the tables.** Four statements contradict material that now sits next to them:

### Summary of key messages
A two-stream network receives the luminance and L−M channels of a cone-opponent image, with a developmental blur and contrast-sensitivity cut on the luminance channel. It ends training with a shape-biased magno stream, supplied by the input and protected by the diet, and a texture-biased parvo stream that is learnt. The four front-end conditions are replicated at three seeds, and the two front-end controls are degenerate on the cortical instrument in every seed. Fitted to eight NSD subjects, the texture-biased stream's unique variance carries every extrastriate region. Its V1 → hV4 gradient is the most extreme of 630 labellings in every seed, and training without the diet erodes it.

Behaviour reads out from both streams. One weight over frozen features moves the cue-conflict rate from 0.40 to 0.90, a refitted nonlinear head preserves the span, and the weight extrastriate cortex prefers (≈ 0.33) is not the weight human choices prefer (0.67).

In 38 frozen ImageNet classifiers with no stream split, one gain on the network's own fifteen-direction shape-discriminant subspace moves the rate by 0.20–0.50 within five points of top-1. The published ranking and the shape-end ranking agree at τ = 0.47, and a probe ranks the zoo differently again (τ = 0.44). A Stylized-ImageNet ResNet-50 is the exception whose bias sits in the representation.

In the authors' two single trunks, extrastriate cortex loads on the texture-leaning half of the units relative to V1. This contrast is absent in the three ImageNet networks fitted, two of them post hoc. The discriminant subspaces carry almost no unique cortical variance in any network. Robustness, trial-level agreement with humans and dimensionality do not follow from the rate. The authors conclude that the cue-conflict rate is a readout statistic and propose three numbers every shape-bias claim should carry.
