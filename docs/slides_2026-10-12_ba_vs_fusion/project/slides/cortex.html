Paper Fig. 3, updated: the texture stream carries ventral cortex

Gradient hV4 − V1: −1.27 vs −0.79 for the BA TSN (8/8 subjects); the adult-diet control is flat (−0.06), like the BA's no-diet network.

NSD, 8 subjects, banded ridge with both streams · BA rows = paper's dkl_bcs / dkl_base, 3 seeds · new TSN 3 seeds · control 1 seed

This is the paper's central cortical result redone on the new TSN, with the paper's own encoding pipeline and statistics. Panel a: for each early visual area, which stream's features add more unique variance to a model that already has the other stream; +1 means only magno adds, −1 only parvo. In the BA TSN the two streams share V1 and parvo takes over towards hV4. The new TSN's gradient is steeper, −1.27 against −0.79, steeper in all 8 subjects: its magno stream now owns V1 (+0.55) and parvo still owns hV4 (−0.76). The networks without a developmental diet, the BA's no-diet twin and our adult-diet-from-the-start control, are magno-dominated everywhere and show no gradient, so the control removes the cortical result along with the behavioural one. The same holds beyond hV4: the ventral, lateral and dorsal regions all fall 1.2 below V1 for the new TSN, 8 of 8 subjects. Panel b: the magno weight of the stream mixture that best predicts each region falls from V1 to extrastriate cortex for both dieted TSNs and rises for both undieted ones. Two more numbers for the paper: the new TSN's cortical fit is higher, median r² over the noise ceiling 0.471 against 0.395, so the diet no longer costs cortical fit; and the in-silico cue-conflict difference between the streams' voxel maps is a little smaller, +1.32 against +1.55.
