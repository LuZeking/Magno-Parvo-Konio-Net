## Consensus Strengths

1. **Rigor is well above the norm for a modelling paper, and NN's checklist is where this
   shows.** Units of replication are correct and never pooled (§4.7); the encoding pipeline
   has no test-set leakage; the noise ceiling is used rather than assumed; four nulls carry
   pre-fixed equivalence bounds; two of four pre-specified predictions failed and are
   reported as failures; nine analyses appear under *Claims deliberately not made*,
   including three the authors previously promoted and retracted.
2. **§4.7 states a statistical limit most papers would hide.** Rather than choosing a
   multiplicity procedure that happens to survive, it proves none can: the exact two-sided
   signed-rank floor at n=8 is 2/2⁸ = .0078125, which exceeds .05/m for every m > 6, so no
   family above six tests can have a single rejection at α = .05. The claims are then
   explicitly rested on complete 8/8 separation, the two-statistics rule, three seeds and
   effect size rather than on corrected significance. R3 regards this as exemplary.
3. **The fusion-weight sweep (§2.3) is a genuine dose–response test of the paper's own
   principle.** Because the stream features do not depend on w, sweeping it moves the
   readout with both components *exactly* frozen; shape bias runs 0.40 → 0.90 monotonically
   in 3/3 seeds at the best checkpoint, and w = 0.5 reproduces the published per-seed values
   to four decimals. This converts the central claim from an argument into a measurement,
   and the authors explicitly decline to present the intermediate value as a prediction that
   could have failed.
4. **The model's stream difference is attributable to input by construction** — 0/120 tensor
   pairs differ at initialisation, and the `rgb_tsn` control returns exactly +0.0000. The
   authors correctly label this an algebraic identity rather than an architecture test.
5. **The single-stream control answers the obvious objection** (§2.3): the split buys 0.088
   shape bias and costs 0.043 accuracy at half the parameters, with complete separation
   under both checkpoint policies, and it fixes the ordering — magno 0.903 > integrated
   0.655 > single 0.567 — so integration is not what destroys the shape bias.

## Consensus Weaknesses

1. **[Scope] The neuroscience payload is a model-comparison result, and the paper says so.**
   §2.2 establishes that a diet-trained model's *unique variance* is distributed differently
   along the ventral hierarchy than a no-diet model's. That is a fact about two models
   fitted to cortex, not about cortex. The paper is admirably explicit ("not a measurement
   of human cortex"; Limitation 9's role-not-content caveat), but the honesty does not
   change what NN is being asked to publish. Compounding it, **§2.7 reports that the diet
   model predicts BOLD *worse*** (median R²/NC 0.405 vs 0.464, p=.0078, 8/8) — so the arm
   carrying the cortical story is the weaker model of cortex.
2. **[§2.2(b)] The load-bearing cortical result is the surviving member of an unregistered
   regional screen, and the disclosure does not travel with it.** The V1v→hV4 gradient is
   **not** in §4.7's pre-registration list (which covers the front-end 2×2, the inverted
   front end, the OOD families, error consistency, the dimensionality ordering and the konio
   colour set). Meanwhile *Claims deliberately not made* records four regional analyses that
   were run and died — hMT+, eccentricity, dorsal/ventral, and VO. The gradient is therefore
   one of at least five regional hypotheses tried, reported as the one that worked, with the
   count disclosed ~40 sections away from the claim. The authors' defences are real and
   substantial — the blur-removal control, monotonicity ρ = −0.964, the global-preference
   control at −0.495 vs +0.849 — but those address **confounds**, not **selection**. NN's
   rigor item 9 asks for the selection quantified against a null; none is reported.
3. **[Methods] Sex as a biological variable is absent — zero mentions in the manuscript.**
   Neither human sample (NSD n=8; Geirhos n=10 observers) has sex reported, and no
   justification is given. NN requires this explicitly, and both source publications carry
   the information. Demographics generally are absent beyond "adults".
4. **[Reporting] Nothing is actually available yet.** Data and code are promised "on
   acceptance"; there are no RRIDs, no software or package versions, and no
   figure→script index. A referee cannot regenerate the headline figure today. NSD and the
   Geirhos stimuli are public, so this is a packaging gap rather than a restriction, but it
   is a gap.
5. **[§4.5] The variance-partitioning instrument is not validated by recovery.** The entire
   §2.2 argument rests on Uₘ/Uₚ, yet no simulation shows the partitioning recovers known
   unique variance under this SNR, ROI size and band correlation. NN's item 23 asks for
   model/parameter recovery for fitted models. This is the single highest-value missing
   analysis and it needs no new data.
6. **[Format] Roughly twice the length, with almost no figures.** Main text is **9,930
   words** against NN's ≈3,000–5,000 — a tighter cap than NHB's — and **13 tables + 2
   figures = 15 display items** against ≤8. Four of the seven result sections are delivered
   entirely as numeric tables. R2 could not follow §2.2 or §2.5 without re-reading.
7. **[§2.1, §2.4, §2.2] Several load-bearing cells remain n=1**: the `swap` and
   `parvo_highsf` cortical fits (seed 0 only), the accuracy-matched control (one run), the
   untrained references (no seed), the konio control (untrained, single seed). Each is
   labelled, which is to the authors' credit, but the Open Items still assert "no n=1 claim
   in its main results", which is not accurate as written.
8. **[Throughout] "Magno" and "parvo" carry anatomical commitments the model does not
   implement** — no temporal dynamics, contrast gain, receptive-field size, or cortical
   targets. §3's "Against channel essentialism" argues this while the labels persist. For an
   NN readership this will read as over-claiming the biology.
9. **[§2.5, §4.1] The developmental framing outruns the manipulation.** The front end is
   static at a fixed 48 months; the one trajectory-trained checkpoint is a different
   codebase and the authors call it the least comparable arm.

## Comments to Authors
==========================================

### Summary of key messages

The manuscript argues that shape-biased behaviour does not require shape-biased
representations, and supports this with a two-stream network fed approximations of the
magnocellular and parvocellular signals. The two streams end training with opposite
shape–texture biases arrived at by different routes — the luminance stream's supplied by
its input, the chromatic stream's built by learning — and, fitted voxelwise to eight NSD
subjects, it is the texture-biased stream whose unique variance carries the ventral end of
the hierarchy. Reweighting the readout over frozen components moves cue-conflict shape bias
from 0.40 to 0.90. Three properties usually assumed to travel with shape bias do not, each
with a pre-fixed equivalence bound.

The standard of self-scrutiny here is high, and I want to say so before the criticism: the
statistical-limit paragraph in §4.7, the retraction of three of your own earlier results,
the reporting of two failed pre-specified predictions, and the explicit labelling of the
two-trunk comparison as an algebraic identity are all things most manuscripts would not do.
My substantive concerns are three: the neuroscience claim is about models fitted to cortex
rather than about cortex; the load-bearing cortical contrast is a screen survivor whose
selection is not quantified; and the instrument that produces it is not validated by
recovery.

### Major comments

**M1.** §2.2 establishes that two models distribute unique explanatory variance differently
along the ventral hierarchy. That is a comparison between models, not a finding about the
ventral stream, and §2.7 reports that the model carrying the story predicts BOLD *worse*
overall (0.405 vs 0.464). Please state plainly, in the Discussion, what a neuroscientist
should conclude about the ventral stream itself from this work — and if the answer is
"nothing directly", say that, because at present the framing invites the stronger reading
while the Limitations withdraw it.

**M2.** The V1v→hV4 gradient does not appear in §4.7's list of pre-specified analyses, while
*Claims deliberately not made* records four other regional analyses that were run and
failed. The gradient is therefore one of at least five regional hypotheses, reported as the
survivor. Your controls — blur removal, ρ = −0.964 monotonicity, the global-preference
contrast — address confounding and are convincing on that score, but none addresses
selection. Please (i) state the number of regional contrasts examined at the point where the
gradient is claimed rather than 40 sections later, and (ii) report the gradient against a
permutation null over hierarchical-rank labels.

**M3.** The variance partitioning is the instrument on which §2.2 entirely depends, and it is
not validated. Please show, in simulation at the SNR, ROI sizes and inter-band correlation
of your data, that the procedure recovers a known Uₘ/Uₚ split and a known gradient — and
that it does not manufacture one from correlated bands. This requires no new data.

**M4.** Sex is not reported for either human sample, and no justification is given. Please
report it for the eight NSD participants and the ten observers, and state whether any result
depends on it.

**M5.** Data and code are promised on acceptance. Please release them now with RRIDs, package
versions and a figure→script index. As it stands a referee cannot regenerate any figure.

**M6.** The main text is roughly twice this journal's length with 15 display items against a
cap of 8, and four result sections are delivered wholly as numeric tables. The w-sweep in
§2.3 in particular is a curve and should be drawn as one.

**M7.** Several results remain n=1 — the `swap` and `parvo_highsf` cortical fits, the
accuracy-matched control, the untrained references, the konio control. Each is labelled in
place, which I appreciate, but the Open Items state that the paper has "no n=1 claim in its
main results", which is not accurate.

**M8.** "Magno" and "parvo" name luminance and L−M inputs to two identical ResNet-18s. No
temporal dynamics, contrast gain, receptive-field size or cortical target is modelled. §3
argues this point under "Against channel essentialism" while the labels do the rhetorical
work throughout. Consider naming the streams by their inputs.

**M9.** The front end is static at a fixed 48 months while the Discussion is headed "What a
developmental constraint actually does". The single trajectory-trained checkpoint is from
another codebase and you describe it as the least comparable arm. Consider framing the
transform as developmentally *motivated* rather than developmental.

### Minor comments

- m1. No software or package versions anywhere; no RRIDs.
- m2. No Reporting Summary provided.
- m3. Undefined on first use for a cross-level readership: DKL, L−M, participation ratio,
  banded ridge, `nsdgeneral`, b3 betas, Uₘ/Uₚ, VSB.
- m4. §2.4's post-hoc exclusion of `parvo_highsf` seed 0 from the behavioural row is
  disclosed but was not pre-specified; please say so at that point.
- m5. Two figure callouts across seven result sections.
- m6. The status header and residual editorial notes should come out before submission.
- m7. The `±` estimator differs between table families (ddof=1 vs ddof=0); you disclose this
  and plan to unify — please do so before submission rather than after.

### Notes by criterion

- **Validity.** No flaw prohibits publication. I checked the items that usually sink
  computational–imaging papers and they are clean: no test-set leakage, ROIs anatomically
  defined, correct replication units, no unvalidated tool. The open validity question is
  M3 — an unvalidated partitioning instrument — not a broken one.
- **Originality and significance.** The specific demonstration is new to my knowledge. The
  underlying principle that readout ≠ representation is not, and the manuscript would be
  stronger for engaging that literature directly rather than presenting the inference as
  unexamined.
- **Data and methodology.** Sound and unusually well documented; not currently reproducible
  from the manuscript alone (M5).
- **Statistics.** Appropriate tests, correct nesting, honest reporting of the exact-test
  floor, equivalence bounds with power caveats. §4.7's refusal to claim corrected
  significance it cannot have is the right call and rare.
- **Conclusions.** Well calibrated and repeatedly scoped, with two exceptions: the framing
  of §2.2 relative to what Limitation 9 withdraws (M1), and the selection question (M2).
- **References.** Now present and appropriate. The premise is correctly sourced to human
  (Jagadeesh & Gardner, 2022) and macaque (Okazawa et al., 2015; Jagadeesh & Livingstone,
  2024) work with the species scope stated — good.
- **Clarity and context.** The four-step argument is well built; length, table density and
  the absence of figures work hard against it.

## Confidential Comments to the Editor
==========================================

**Triage.** Discuss, leaning transfer. This is a careful, honest, technically strong paper
whose central claim is not the kind Nature Neuroscience exists to publish. The conceptual
payload is a principle about how to interpret a *behavioural* measure of representation; the
cortical work serves to locate which model component explains the ventral end. Our own
transfer guidance covers this case exactly — *behaviour/cognition is the claim, neural data
secondary* — and points to Nature Human Behaviour.

**Significance.** Against our heuristics I count about one and a half of six. It arguably
"establishes a principle that generalises beyond the preparation" and would change what some
investigators infer from a behavioural rate. It does not identify a mechanism the field must
incorporate, does not resolve a controversy decisively for the nervous system, and enables
no new capability. That is a borderline, and R1 votes "no" outright.

**Recommendation.** Major revision by the arithmetic (6.09), but I want the editor to see
the split: individually R1 = 5.71 and R2 = 5.81, both inside the transfer band, while R3 =
6.74. **The rigor referee likes it most.** That is the signature of a well-executed paper at
the wrong venue, not of a weak paper. If the authors are set on this journal, the revision
that would change my view is not cosmetic: lead with the cortical result as a claim about
cortex, which would require reconciling it with §2.7's finding that the diet model is the
*worse* model of BOLD — and I do not think the data support that lead.

**What the revision needs regardless of venue,** in priority order: (i) recovery validation
of the variance partitioning — this is the one analysis whose absence I regard as a real
validity gap, and it is CPU-only; (ii) the regional family size stated at the claim plus a
selection null; (iii) sex reported for both samples; (iv) code and data released now; (v) a
figure programme and a cut to length.

**No new experiments are required.** All of the above is analysis and writing. Revision
time 2–3 months.

**A note on conduct.** This manuscript retracts three of its own earlier results, reports
two of four pre-specified predictions as failures, corrects errors from its own previous
version in visible footnotes, and proves in §4.7 that a statistical guarantee it would like
to claim is unavailable to it. Several of my criticisms above exist because the authors
disclosed the underlying facts. That deserves weight.

**Expertise.** High on human visual neuroscience, encoding models and model–brain
comparison; moderate on developmental vision. **Conflicts:** none.
